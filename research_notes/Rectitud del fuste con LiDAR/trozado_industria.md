# Stem sweep in log merchandising, bucking optimisation and value recovery, and LiDAR stem-quality inventory in Australian and NZ softwood plantations

Compiled 19 Nov 2026 for PS_LiDAR (HQPlantations, PHYB hybrid pine). ## Q1. How much does sweep reduce log outturn and value (sawn timber, veneer/peeler/LVL, Queensland pines)?

### Takeaway
Sweep is one of the largest single geometric drivers of log value. The best-known figures: in NZ, pruned radiata butt logs with average sweep (13 mm/m) gave about 20% less sawn output than straight logs. In Australian radiata, a 10% reduction in sweep cut sawlog degrade by 17%. For spindleless rotary veneer (Queensland DAF work), sweep had the biggest effect of any log-geometry variable: 0.01 m/m of sweep lowered the break-even mill-delivered log price by about $20/m³, and 0.04–0.08 m/m made small logs unprofitable to peel. I found no published quantification for slash, Caribbean or hybrid pine in Queensland.

### Cited Findings
**Sawn timber, NZ radiata (old but foundational)**
- Cown, McConchie & Treloar (1984), *NZ J. For. Sci.* 14(1):109–123, "Timber recovery from pruned *Pinus radiata* butt logs at **Mangatu**: effect of log sweep". (The site is Mangatu, not Mohaka.) Sixty 5.5-m pruned butt logs from two stands. Average swept logs (mean sweep **13 mm/m**) yielded **20% less sawn output** than straighter logs. A severely swept class (**30 mm/m**) was also reported, and increasing sweep markedly reduced recovery of **clear cuttings longer than 3 m**. [PR][SNIPPET: abstract only; Scion's PDF is behind a Cloudflare challenge] — [Scion PDF](https://www.scionresearch.com/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/59701/NZJFS1411984COWN109_123.pdf)
- A later NZJFS paper (Turner et al. 1999, 29(1):146–164) reuses Cown et al. (1984) in a valuation and cites average log values for **moderately (11–20 mm/m)** and **severely** swept logs. [PR][SNIPPET] — [NZJFS 29(1) PDF](https://www.scionresearch.com/__data/assets/pdf_file/0007/59461/NZJFS291_1999_146_164TURNER.pdf)
- A search snippet stated that "complete elimination from this site of all stems having sweep would result in a value recovery gain of four per cent". It comes from a different NZ study, and I could not identify its exact source. [UNVERIFIED] — [search hit list incl. NZJFS 1988 Cown](https://www.scionresearch.com/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/59900/NZJFS1831988COWN345_358.pdf)

**Sawn structural timber, Australian radiata**
- Ivković, Wu, Spencer & McRae (2007), "Modelling the effects of stem sweep, branch size and wood stiffness of radiata pine on structural timber production", *Australian Forestry* 70(3):173–184, doi:10.1080/00049158.2007.10675018. The model is built on Australian industry data. Per the abstract, a **10% reduction in sweep reduced sawlog degrade by 17.1%** and raised green timber recovery by about **0.5%**. A 10% reduction in branch size cut degraded sawlog volume by 68% and raised structural timber recovery by 0.6–1.6%. [PR][SNIPPET: abstract only] — [Scilit record](https://www.scilit.net/publications/5f28defbc2ffa86062b641085760ff1b); [BibBase](https://bibbase.org/network/publication/ivkovi-wu-spencer-mcrae-modellingtheeffectsofstemsweepbranchsizeandwoodstiffnessofradiatapineonstructuraltimberproduction-2007)
- A companion series (Ivković, Powell, McRae & Wu 2006, and Ivković, Wu, McRae & Matheson 2006 in *Can. J. For. Res.*) puts **stem sweep** among the breeding-objective traits (MAI, sweep, branch size, MoE) and links it to bioeconomic production-system models. [PR][SNIPPET] — [CAS catalogue record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi...........428fa3ae5ec0250db3bc69fb5934d338); [BibBase 2006](https://bibbase.org/network/publication/ivkovi-wu-mcrae-matheson-developingbreedingobjectivesforradiatapinestructuralwoodproductioniisensitivityanalyses-2006)

**Veneer / peeler logs (Australian, Queensland DAF co-author)**
- Venn, McGavin & Ergashev (2020), *BioResources* 15(2):2385–2411, "Accommodating log dimensions and geometry in log procurement decisions for spindleless rotary veneer production" [PR] — [PDF](https://bioresources.cnr.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/BioRes_15_2_2385_Venn_ME_Accomodating_Log_Dimen_Geometry_Log_Procur_Spindleless_Veneer_16815-1.pdf); Qld DAF eRA copy: [era.dpi.qld.gov.au/7570](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/7570/1/16815-62752-1-PB.pdf)
  - Ranked by impact on net recovery of marketable veneer: **sweep > SEDUB > taper > ovality**.
  - Relative to a cylindrical **2.6-m** log, sweep of **0.01 m/m lowered MDLCmax** (maximum mill-delivered log cost that still meets a target gross margin) **by $20/m³**. Taper of 0.01 m/m lowered it by $10/m³.
  - At a $2000/h gross margin, 0.01 m/m sweep lowered MDLCmax by **$19/m³** for 20-cm SEDUB logs and **$17/m³** for 60-cm logs. At **0.04 m/m**, 20-cm SEDUB logs earned no positive margin and 60-cm logs lost **$62/m³**. At **0.08 m/m**, 30-cm SEDUB logs earned no positive margin.
  - Mechanism: irregular billets are rounded to a cylinder before veneer is recovered, so sweep is lost as round-up waste. Veneer prices in the model were Australian plantation-softwood veneer prices. The time study used 164 eucalypt logs.
  - Taper example for scale: a 30-cm SEDUB log with 0.02 m/m taper gave 49% net recovery against 59% for a cylinder, i.e. 20% less veneer. Sweep's effect was larger still.

**Scandinavian evidence on bucking value (recent)**
- Skogforsk Arbetsrapport 1275-2026 (Lindgren, Hannrup, Möller et al., with Komatsu Forest, RISE and SLU) [GREY, 2026] — [PDF](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/382d558622a34fc5989b895e686135d6/1275-2026-matning-av-krok-och-virkeskvalitet-med-mobila-sensorer-pa-skordare.pdf)
  - For Scots pine, sweep is the most common reason Swedish sawmills downgrade or reject logs. Harvesters have no tool to measure sweep before felling, so the operator judges it by eye.
  - Earlier sawing simulations found that bucking on 3D stem form can raise sawn-timber value by **about 4–6%** (Nordmark 2005; Rummukainen et al. 2021, as cited).
  - Worked example: a tree bucked conventionally lost its crooked butt log to reject, leaving one sawlog worth **112.8 SEK**. Crook-aware bucking deliberately cut a short pulp log over the crooked section and recovered two straight sawlogs above it, worth **156.5 SEK**. This is one tree, not an average.
  - The scaling rule matters. The newer VMR 07 rule (yield loss, "utbytesförlust") is stricter and produces more rejects than the older VMR 99 rule (bow height as % of log length).
- Prendes, Acuna (University of the Sunshine Coast, USC), Canga, Ordoñez & Cabo (2023), *Scand. J. For. Res.* 38(3):174–188, "Optimal bucking of stems from terrestrial laser scanning data to maximize forest value". Its main finding is that **leaving curvature out of the bucking algorithm overestimates the commercial value** of timber products. [PR][SNIPPET] — [USC record](https://research.usc.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Optimal-bucking-of-stems-from-terrestrial/99728797202621)

**Effect of sweep code on predicted log mix (NZ inventory practice)**
- Interpine (2013) compared two otherwise similar trees, one coded "Long" sweep and one "Short" sweep. The predicted value differed by about **10%**. Without a "Longs" code, the yield model over-predicts long-log volume. [VENDOR, 2013] — [Interpine blog](https://interpine.nz/better-predicting-yield-of-long-logs-vs-short-logs-of-the-same-grade-quality/)

**Queensland slash, Caribbean and hybrid pine (indirect only)**
- In Queensland breeding work, Caribbean pine (PCH) has poorer stem straightness and wind-firmness than slash pine (PEE). The F1 hybrid is described as combining slash pine's density, **straightness** and wind-firmness with PCH's growth and finer branching. Hybrid straightness can be selected from pure-PEE progeny-trial data. Slash pine breeding has also used a 3-point utilisation score at age 6 (1 = pulp, 2 = pulp + sawlog, 3 = sawlog) that reflects straightness, size and branching. [PR/GREY][SNIPPET: these statements came back from a search across these Qld DAF eRA documents; I did not confirm which document says what] — [Selecting hybrid pine clones (Qld DAF eRA)](https://era.daf.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/11529/1/Selecting%20hybrid%20pine%20clones%20for%20deployment%20-%20The%20pointy%20end%20of%20wood%20quality%20improvement.pdf); [Correlations pure/hybrid combining abilities of slash pine](https://era.daf.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/10676/1/Correlations%20between%20pure%20and%20hybrid%20combining%20abilities%20of%20slash%20pine%20parents.pdf); [Southern pines (eRA)](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/5800/1/Southern%20pines_for%20eRA.pdf)
- FWPA PNC361-1416 (southern pines resource characterisation, USC/DAF, about 2019) focuses on stiffness (MoE), not sweep. Searching its text for "sweep" found nothing relevant. [GREY] — [PNC361 final report](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/7939/1/Final_Report_Southern_Pines_PNC361-1416.pdf)

### Inferences
- The value penalty is strongly nonlinear and depends on log length and product. It is small below roughly 5–10 mm/m. It becomes large in short, small-diameter logs (veneer) and in long sawlogs. A classifier should therefore resolve the decision boundaries that set log length (e.g. "Long" vs "Short" single sweep), not just "straight vs swept".
- In veneer, sweep matters most for small-SED logs (the low-value end typical of thinnings and PHYB peeler logs). For HQP peeler/LVL assortments, small differences in sweep around the specification limit could change the product outcome.
- Where sweep sits along the stem matters as much as its magnitude. The Skogforsk example and Prendes et al. (2023) show that crook-aware bucking recovers value by cutting around the defect. A per-section sweep description (the PS_LiDAR output design) is therefore more useful than a single tree-level score.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed quantification found of sweep vs sawn or veneer recovery for **slash, Caribbean or hybrid pine** in Queensland, nor for HQP log grades. The likely sources are unpublished HQP/DAF mill studies or Qld DAF Salisbury veneer trials.
- Full texts of Cown et al. (1984) and Ivković et al. (2007) were not accessible (Cloudflare / paywall). The model details behind the 17.1% figure, and the definition of the sweep unit used, are unconfirmed.
- I could not open Benyon's ANU thesis "The relative economic importance of stem straightness, branch diameter and stem diameter in *P. radiata* breeding" (PDF over 10 MB). It may hold Australian economic weights for straightness. — [ANU repository](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstream/1885/143322/2/b1748067x_Benyon_R_G.pdf)
- No sweep-specific figure found for **radiata** veneer or LVL with spindle lathes (e.g. Nelson Pine, Carter Holt Harvey).

## Q2. Which bucking and value-optimisation tools exist, and how do they consume sweep and stem-quality descriptions?

### Takeaway
The Australian and NZ inventory chain is **cruiser stem description → yield table generator / bucking simulator → estate model**. Concretely: MARVL / PlotSafe-style feature cruising, then YTGen or Atlas, then Woodstock/Remsoft or similar. Sweep enters as a **categorical code per stem segment**, not as millimetres. Each log grade is defined by which sweep codes it accepts, and a dynamic-programming bucker chooses cut positions. The open-source optimisers (BuckR 2025, optBuck) work from diameters, lengths and prices and **do not model sweep**. Research optimisers on TLS stems (Prendes et al. 2023; Skogforsk 2026) do add curvature constraints per candidate log.

### Cited Findings
**MARVL (Scion / NZ FRI)**
- Deadman & Goulding (1979), *NZ J. For. Sci.* 9(2):225–239. MARVL (Method of Assessing Recoverable Volume by Log type) estimates recoverable volume by log type for a stand shortly before harvest. It samples trees, records **stem size, quality and malformation as one-letter feature codes**, and keeps separate fixed **structural codes** (e.g. F = fork) for features that always affect volume or force a cut. A **dynamic-programming optimisation** then estimates the potential yield of each stem. Because the stem description is stored, the same data can be re-bucked against different log specifications without returning to the stand. The method was tested at 10 clear-fell sites. [PR][SNIPPET] — [Scion PDF](https://www.scionresearch.com/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/59144/NZJFS921979DEADMAN225_239.pdf)

**Interpine PlotSafe + YTGen (the chain HQP's PlotSafe-format output feeds)**
- **Dictionaries.** NZ MARVL inventories commonly use the cruising dictionaries **RAD05, RAD05-A and RAD06**. Their domains are **Br** (branch), **Sw** (sweep), **F** (features) and **I** (internode). Branch-domain breaks in cm: RAD05 0/1/7/10/12/25/99; RAD05-A 0/1/4/7/10/12/25/99; RAD06 0/1/4/7/10/14/20/99. A branch code that does not match the cutting-strategy template makes YTGen skip grades and distorts yields. [VENDOR, 2013] — [Interpine: RAD05/05A/06](https://interpine.nz/marvl-cruising-dictionaries-what-is-rad05-rad05-a-and-rad06/)
- **Longs vs Shorts sweep codes** split one sweep class (e.g. "**SED 1/4 assessed over 6 m**") into two codes:
  - "Shorts" (S): a gentle single sweep over about 6 m, but a little sweep in more than one direction beyond 6 m.
  - "Longs" (L): a single gentle sweep moving in one consistent direction.

  A stem acceptable at SED/4 every 6 m may fail at 8 or 12 m because of double sweep. Other conventions in the post:
  - NZ: Shorts are sawlogs ≤ 6.1 m; Longs run up to 12 m for export grades. The RAD05 domain file says Longs go "up to 12 m going on SED/2".
  - Example grade: Sw:8,L, with Log-Long lengths of 7.4, 8.1 and 12.1 m.
  - Wobble must be described when severe. Mild sinuosity should not be coded as wobble or as a short section of SED/3.
  - The split dates from late-1990s MARVL dictionaries.
  - The post's Australian length convention ("Longs up to 4.6 m, Shorts up to 6.1 m") reads as inconsistent and should be checked with Interpine.

  [VENDOR, 2013] — [Interpine: long vs short logs](https://interpine.nz/better-predicting-yield-of-long-logs-vs-short-logs-of-the-same-grade-quality/)
- **YTGen cutting strategy (.YTS)** — [VENDOR, 2013] — [Interpine: YTGen cutting strategy](https://interpine.nz/understanding-a-ytgen-cutting-strategy-from-marvl-to-logmaking/)
  - Grades are defined by length, diameter (including min/max mid-diameter) and quality conditions.
  - **Sweep (Sw) is a string, usually a "must have" condition, and must also list every higher-quality code.** Example: a grade accepting SED/4 is written **`SW:8,L,S`**, so SED/8 (the straightest class) is included.
  - Branches are numeric "must have" (e.g. `Br<=7`). Features are "must not have" (e.g. `F!:F5+,F10+`).
  - Structural codes (fork `<`, broken top `*`, reduction `>`, forced cut `%`) are generally allowed only in pulp. Sound dead trees go to pulp only. Live windblown trees are excluded from all grades.
  - `$CD` "chocolate dip" handles part-pruned logs (e.g. 3.7 m pruned).
  - YTGen's main output is yield estimates for user-defined log grades over one or more periods. — [Interpine: what is YTGen](https://interpine.nz/so-what-is-ytgen-generating-forest-yield-predictions/)
- A PlotSafe sweep dictionary used on Australian (FCNSW Carabost, NSW) radiata data in FWPA PNC464 ("Interpine's PlotSafe Overlapping Feature Cruising, Forest Inventory Procedures, February 2007") lists:
  - **6** = straight
  - **L** = gentle sweep over 6 m
  - **S** = gentle sweep over 4 m
  - **2** = moderate sweep
  - **1** = excessive sweep
  - **W** = wobble back and forth
  - **K** = kink (sharp change of direction)

  [GREY, 2019] — [FWPA PNC464-1718](https://fwpa.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Amended_Final_Report_PNC464-1718.pdf)
- **PlotSafe audit demerits** (PNC464, from the PlotSafe guidelines and Interpine auditor Bruce Hill):
  - Tree demerits are split into **Structural** (sweep codes 1, W, K; forks) and **Quality** (sweep codes 2, S, L, 6; branch size; spikes; damage).
  - Structural errors are weighted **twice** quality errors, and both weights fall with height on the stem:

    | Height on stem | Structural | Quality |
    |---|---|---|
    | 0–12 m | 6 | 3 |
    | 12–20 m | 4 | 2 |
    | > 20 m | 2 | 1 |

  - Height tolerance is ±1.0 m, then 2 demerits per 0.5 m beyond it.

  [GREY, 2019] — [FWPA PNC464-1718](https://fwpa.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Amended_Final_Report_PNC464-1718.pdf)

**Atlas Cruiser / Atlas Forecaster**
- Murphy, Acuna & Dumbrell (2010), *Can. J. For. Res.* 40(11):2223–2233. Stems from **Atlas Cruiser** inventory procedures, from TLS, and from manual post-harvest measurement were **optimally bucked to Australian log specifications and prices** in radiata (33 plots, 6 stands). Results against actual harvester recovery:
  - Cruiser volume and value estimates: within **4%**.
  - TLS volume and value: within **8% and 7%**, and only for 5 of 6 stands.

  How sweep was handled is not stated in the abstract. [PR][SNIPPET] — [USC record](https://research.usc.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Tree-value-and-log-product-yield/99450398902621)

**Open-source dynamic-programming buckers**
- **BuckR** (Bennemann, Lussier & Labelle 2025), *Forests* 16(5):780, written in R. [PR] — [MDPI PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/forests/forests-16-00780/article_deploy/forests-16-00780.pdf)
  - Uses the classic Dykstra DP formulation and reproduces Dykstra's example. Inputs are tree data plus product specifications with prices. The objective is tree value, and can be switched to volume.
  - An `rpart` classifier maps (log length, SED) to a ProductID.
  - **Sweep and defects are not implemented.** The authors list "consider stem defects and sweep criteria" as a possible modification. It also does not handle the harvester's partial-information case.
  - The paper notes that automated CTL harvester bucking "does not consider value-conditioning stem defects or damages, such as knots, rot, or crooks".
- **optBuck** (NIBIO / SmartForest Norway; L. Noordermeer). R package with a Shiny app.
  - Finds the highest-value cutting pattern per stem under products and prices. Reads and writes **StanForD 2010 `.hpr` (production) and `.pin` (product instruction)** files.
  - Can compare recorded harvester bucking with optimal bucking via `bucking_check()` and `buck_hpr()`. Uses Norwegian taper models (taperNOR). MIT licence.
  - The README does not mention sweep or quality. [VENDOR/OPEN-SOURCE] — [GitHub README](https://github.com/SmartForest-no/optBuck); [rdrr mirror](https://rdrr.io/github/lennartnoordermeer/optBuck/f/README.md)

**Curvature-aware research buckers**
- Prendes et al. (2023): a TLS-based optimal bucker using 3D stem models that **includes the diameter and curvature of each candidate log**. Per Skogforsk's description, it applies different curvature limits for different products, e.g. veneer logs. [PR][SNIPPET] — [USC record](https://research.usc.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Optimal-bucking-of-stems-from-terrestrial/99728797202621); [Skogforsk 1275-2026](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/382d558622a34fc5989b895e686135d6/1275-2026-matning-av-krok-och-virkeskvalitet-med-mobila-sensorer-pa-skordare.pdf)
- Skogforsk 2026 evaluated each candidate log's curvature as bow height, radius of curvature and yield loss. Bow height was much faster to compute than yield loss. [GREY] — [Skogforsk 1275-2026](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/382d558622a34fc5989b895e686135d6/1275-2026-matning-av-krok-och-virkeskvalitet-med-mobila-sensorer-pa-skordare.pdf)
- Hardwood DP buckers that model curvature: **HW-Buck** (Pickens et al. 1991; potential value gains of 39–55%) and **CAHO** (Wang et al. 2009; DP governed by grading rules). Both are reviewed in BuckR's Table 1. [PR] — [BuckR PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/forests/forests-16-00780/article_deploy/forests-16-00780.pdf)
- Marshall & Murphy (Scion) simulated bucking with scanning for Douglas-fir and ponderosa pine. Net value change against conventional bucking ranged from **−7% to +8%** after costs, and full pre-bucking scanning was best. [PR][SNIPPET] — [Scion PDF](https://www.scionresearch.com/__data/assets/pdf_file/0007/59164/03_Marshall_Murphy.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The classifier's real output is a per-segment sweep code that a YTGen-style grade definition can test.** Each code amounts to a maximum allowable deviation over a stated length: SED/n over 4 m (S) or 6 m (L), with "Long" meaning single-direction sweep that still passes over 8–12 m. The numeric codes appear to encode the maximum sweep as a fraction of SED (8 → SED/8, 3 → SED/3, 2 → SED/2). This reading follows from `SW:8,L,S` meaning "SED/4 and better", but HQP's exact thresholds for 8/L/S/3/1/X/W/K must come from the **HQP LiDAR Feature Cruising Dictionary**, not from NZ RAD05.
- Because grades accept *sets* of codes ("must have, inclusive of higher quality"), a confusion between adjacent classes (8↔L, L↔S) only costs value when it crosses a grade's acceptance boundary. Errors involving structural classes (1, W, K, X) are costly, because they can force a stem to pulp or hide a pulp-only section. The PlotSafe demerit matrix (structural = 2× quality, weighted towards the butt) is a ready-made, industry-recognised **cost-sensitive evaluation metric**.
- A geometric output is also worth keeping alongside the code: maximum sagitta per segment, the segment length it applies over, single vs multiple direction, and the position of kinks. With it, (a) a different dictionary or new log specification can be re-coded later without rescanning, the same argument Deadman & Goulding made for MARVL, and (b) a curvature-aware DP bucker such as a modified BuckR can run directly on the geometry.
- Estate planners (Remsoft Woodstock and the like) consume **stand-level yield tables by log product**, not stem descriptions. Errors in the sweep classifier therefore reach estate planning only through YTGen/Atlas log-mix tables, i.e. as bias in the sawlog/peeler/pulp proportions. This is an inference; I did not confirm it from Remsoft documentation.

### Gaps
- I found no public documentation of **Atlas Cruiser / Atlas Forecaster** stem-description or sweep coding (Atlas Technology, NZ), nor of AVIS.
- **HQP's own dictionary** (code thresholds for 8/L/S/3/1/X/W/K) and HQP's cutting strategies and log specifications for PHYB (sawlog, peeler, pulp) are not public.
- No public source shows **Remsoft/Woodstock** taking stem-level sweep. I did not search SilviaTerra, Treemetrics or Silmetra product documentation in depth. I found nothing linking **Silmetra** to PlotSafe or HQP.
- The YTGen log-making algorithm (DP vs priority list) is not described in the Interpine posts. MARVL is DP (Deadman & Goulding 1979), and YTGen is understood to be MARVL-derived, but that is not confirmed here.

## Q3. Who in Australia and NZ is doing LiDAR-based stem-quality inventory, with what methods and results?

### Takeaway
Industry practice in Australia and NZ (late 2026) is airborne or UAV LiDAR for stand-level inventory and stocking, plus backpack SLAM (mostly Emesent Hovermap) on plots for DBH, height, volume and stem profiles. **Sweep and quality codes are still assigned mainly by human cruisers**: in the field, or (in R&D) on point clouds in VR. I found no published, validated, operational automated sweep-coding system in Australia or NZ. The main actors are Interpine (NZ; PlotSafe/YTGen; Hovermap since 2019–20), Scion and Forest Growers Research, the University of Sydney's ACFR (Mitch Bryson, with HQP data), UTas, NSW DPI (Christine Stone), UniSA/NIFPI, and USC's Forest Research Institute (Mauricio Acuna).

### Cited Findings
**FWPA / NIFPI programme lineage (Australia)**
- **PRC281-1112 (June 2012).** Australia's six largest pine plantation managers, including HQPlantations, formed a collaborative to coordinate LiDAR deployment. The report discusses later-age, post-first-thinning pre-harvest LiDAR inventory. [GREY] — [FWPA PRC281](https://fwpa.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/PRC281-1112_Operational_deployment_of_LiDAR_in_Australian_softwood_plantations_0.pdf)
- **PNC305-1213 (Feb 2015).** Evaluated area-based LiDAR inventory for softwood plantations (imputation, stocking methods). Sweep is not addressed. [GREY] — [FWPA PNC305](https://fwpa.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/FWPA_PNC305-1213_Final_Report_Feb2015_amended.pdf)
- **PNC377-1516 (June 2018; editors M. Watt, Scion, and C. Stone, NSW DPI)**, a Trans-Tasman project. [GREY] — [FWPA PNC377](https://fwpa.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/PNC377-1516_Final_Report_27_Sept18-.pdf)
  - Data: helicopter Riegl VUX-1LR and UAV dense point clouds over pre-harvest *P. radiata*. Field reference collected with Interpine **PlotSafe** (tree cruising, including "Tree quality assessment – Sweep & Branching").
  - Scion's TLS tool **Cloud2Stem**, plus a RANSAC **cubic-spline stem centreline** "that can account for sweep and lean", was tested on stands of about 400 stems/ha.
  - Conclusion: "Currently it is not possible for the algorithms presented here to produce log product estimates", because **sweep, major-branch position, defects and vitality** had not been extracted. Sweep "could potentially be measured from the cubic spline curves".
  - The report also suggests deep learning for "automated stem quality assessments including detection of multi-leaders, sweep and position of major branches".
- **PNC464-1718 (Nov 2019)**, "Enhanced forest inventory practice using immersive visualisation and measurement of dense point cloud data". [GREY] — [FWPA PNC464](https://fwpa.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Amended_Final_Report_PNC464-1718.pdf)
  - Team: UTas HIT Lab (Chinthammit, Osborn), NSW DPI (Stone), Interpine (Herries, Hill, Gonzalez). Steering committee: FCNSW, FPC WA, **HQPlantations (Kevin Cooney, Lee Stamm)**, STTas, PF Olsen.
  - Data: helicopter LiDAR (Feb 2018) over FCNSW radiata at Carabost SF.

## Q4. Can harvester-head data (StanForD 2010 `.hpr` stem profiles) serve as ground truth for sweep, and has pre-harvest LiDAR been compared with harvester data?

### Takeaway
Harvester files are a good ground truth for **diameter profiles, log lengths and the products actually cut**, i.e. outturn and value. They are a poor ground truth for **sweep geometry**: `.hpr` stores diameters along the stem axis, not its 3D centreline, and current harvesters cannot measure sweep. In Australia and NZ, pre-harvest LiDAR has been checked against harvester recovery for volume and value, not for sweep. The best direct sweep ground truths are TLS against CT (Skogforsk 2026) and TLS against sawmill or log-scanner geometry (Pyörälä 2019).

### Cited Findings
- **What `.hpr` contains.** StanForD 2010 `.hpr` files carry the **diameter vector of the commercial part of the stem**, typically at **10-cm intervals** from the first measurement point. That point sits 20–130 cm above the stump depending on the head, and the standard assumes a 10-cm stump. Butt diameters are often reconstructed. The most advanced heads record at about 1-cm intervals. [PR] — [Silva Fennica supplement](https://silvafennica.fi/supp_files/article25014/S1.pdf); [J. For. Res. 2017](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11676-017-0517-1.pdf); [Skogforsk StanForD 2010 intro](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/1a68cdce4af1462ead048b7a5ef1cc06/stanford-2010-introduction-200820.pdf)
- **Harvesters do not measure sweep.** "Current harvesters lack technical tools to objectively measure sweep prior to felling"; the operator judges it by eye. Feed-roller slip also distorts bow-height comparisons between CT and TLS. [GREY, 2026] — [Skogforsk 1275-2026](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/382d558622a34fc5989b895e686135d6/1275-2026-matning-av-krok-och-virkeskvalitet-med-mobila-sensorer-pa-skordare.pdf)
- **Automated bucking ignores crook.** Automated CTL harvester bucking "does not consider … crooks". [PR] — [BuckR, Forests 2025](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/forests/forests-16-00780/article_deploy/forests-16-00780.pdf)
- **Australian comparisons with harvester recovery (volume and value only)**
  - Murphy et al. (2010): TLS vs Atlas Cruiser vs harvester recovery in Australian radiata (TLS within 8% volume and 7% value; cruiser within 4%). [PR] — [USC record](https://research.usc.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Tree-value-and-log-product-yield/99450398902621)
  - Caccamo et al. (2018), *Australian Forestry* 81, compared yield estimates from LiDAR and aerial-photogrammetric point clouds with **cut-to-length harvester data** in Tasmanian *P. radiata*. Sweep is not part of the comparison as cited. [PR][SNIPPET via PNC377 reference list] — [doi:10.1080/00049158.2018.1458582](https://doi.org/10.1080/00049158.2018.1458582)
- **Skogforsk 2026: sweep against CT and TLS** [GREY] — [Skogforsk 1275-2026](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/382d558622a34fc5989b895e686135d6/1275-2026-matning-av-krok-och-virkeskvalitet-med-mobila-sensorer-pa-skordare.pdf)
  - TLS bow height against **CT scanning** (10 trees, logs of about 4 m): bias / RMSE of 0.2 / 0.7 cm (log 1), −0.4 / 0.8 cm (log 2), −0.1 / 0.8 cm (log 3), −1.0 / 1.5 cm (log 4). Mean CT bow height was 4.0, 1.5, 1.6 and 1.1 cm.
  - **Handheld Hovermap MLS against TLS:** RMSE **4.4 mm**, bias 1.8 mm for 1–5 m. RMSE 17 mm for 5–10 m, where cross-crook and double tops broke the algorithms.
  - **Harvester-mounted MLS against TLS**, 1–5 m: RMSE 9.1 mm (wide FOV); 29 mm, or 8.5 mm without one outlier tree (narrow FOV). Mean bow height was about 17 mm, so most butt logs were near-straight. Stems were detected best around 3 m above ground; 288 TLS reference trees.
- **Pyörälä et al. (2019)**, *Forestry* 92(2):177–187: 52 Scots pines, TLS log geometry against **sawmill measurements**. Diameter, volume and taper agreed reasonably well, but **sweep was poorly estimated (relative mean difference about 78%)**. [PR][SNIPPET] — [UTU record](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/458274175?lang=en_US)
- **Older Swedish work:** Skogforsk Report 828 (2014) evaluated TLS taper and volume against harvester and manual measurements. A 2022 *Croatian J. For. Eng.* study fed TLS-fitted taper curves into a bucking optimiser and compared the result with operator cuts. Both concern taper, not sweep. [GREY/PR][SNIPPET] — [Skogforsk 828](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/5f0beee7c98846e0aadfa6391c6aa578/evaluation-of-single-tree-based-estimates-with-terrestrial-laser-scanning-in-sweden.pdf); [CROJFE 43(2)](https://crojfe.com/archive/volume-43-no.2/stem-level-bucking-pattern-optimization-in-chainsaw-bucking-based-on-terrestrial-laser-scanning-data/)
- optBuck can re-buck the measured `.hpr` stems under the file's own product instruction (`buck_hpr`, `bucking_check`). That makes it a ready tool for **outcome-level** validation: predicted log mix against what was cut. [OPEN-SOURCE] — [optBuck](https://github.com/SmartForest-no/optBuck)

### Inferences
- For PS_LiDAR, validation can run at two levels:
  - **Geometric:** TLS/MLS centreline sweep against a reference. Use TLS on a subset, felled-log measurement, or mill log-scanner 3D data where HQP mills have scanners.
  - **Outcome:** predicted log mix from YTGen/optBuck on the LiDAR stem description against the `.hpr` actual products for the same trees. This needs tree matching through harvester GNSS, or a plot-level comparison.
- `.hpr` products and cut positions indirectly reveal sweep-driven decisions. A sawlog grade cut short, or a pulp section cut out mid-stem, is one signature. But the operator's bucking also reflects price lists and mill orders, so treat it as a noisy label.

### Gaps
- No Australian or NZ study found that compares **pre-harvest LiDAR sweep** with harvester or mill outcomes for the same trees.
- I did not verify whether any harvester makes used in Queensland (e.g. Ponsse, John Deere, Komatsu, Waratah, Log Max heads) record operator quality or crook codes in `.hpr` in a form usable as sweep labels.

## Q5. How accurate is automated sweep assessment compared with manual (cruiser) assessment?

### Takeaway
The evidence is thin and mostly outside Australia and NZ. Algorithms recover stem-centreline curvature to millimetre or centimetre level on the lower stem (TLS/MLS, 1–5 m). Agreement with **visual categorical classes** is weak, because visual classes overlap heavily. Human cruisers working on point clouds in VR (Australian data) missed most wobble and the upper-stem structural sweep. I found no published Australian or NZ industry trial reporting agreement statistics between automated sweep codes and cruiser codes.

### Cited Findings
- **Human cruisers on LiDAR in VR (PNC464, 2019, Australian radiata).** Seventeen experienced cruisers assessed 6 trees:
  - **13 of 17 missed wobble** below 12 m.
  - Above 20 m, they missed "1" (excessive sweep), a structural error.
  - Above 12 m, forks and spikes could not be identified reliably.
  - Of these users, 50% or more rated sweep estimation in VR as neutral or difficult. The likely cause is insufficient point density on the stem and point rendering without surfaces.
  - Height in VR came within ±1 m of field measurement for 5 of 6 trees. Manual circle-fitted DBH was strongly biased high: 594 vs 461 mm and 495 vs 345 mm.

  [GREY] — [FWPA PNC464](https://fwpa.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Amended_Final_Report_PNC464-1718.pdf)
- **Prendes et al. (2022), *Forests* 13(3):431** (*P. pinaster* breeding trial, TLS; USC co-author). [PR] — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4907/13/3/431)
  - Method: fully automatic estimation of **maximum sagitta, standardised maximum sagitta (SSmax), sinuosity and lean** from evenly spaced section centres (e.g. 23 sections over 0.5–4.9 m).
  - Against operators measuring section centres manually: operator–operator SSmax differences were below 5 cm in 99% of cases; algorithm–operator differences below 10 cm in 92%. TLS tended to give larger sagittas.
  - Against **visual 3-class straightness**: mean SSmax was **0.095 m (class 1, n = 272), 0.139 m (class 2, n = 108) and 0.144 m (class 3, n = 5)**. Distributions overlapped strongly, and there was **no significant difference** between classes (Tukey, α = 0.05).
  - Conclusion: visual classes are poorly defined, and the automatic method "compares favorably" with traditional field techniques, which often misclassify.
- **Pyörälä et al. (2019):** sweep from TLS against sawmill reference had a relative mean difference of about 78% (Scots pine, 52 trees). [PR][SNIPPET] — [UTU record](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/458274175?lang=en_US)
- **Skogforsk (2026)** (see Q4): Hovermap bow height against TLS had RMSE 4.4 mm at 1–5 m and 17 mm at 5–10 m. Existing algorithms cannot handle cross-crook or double tops. [GREY] — [Skogforsk 1275-2026](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/382d558622a34fc5989b895e686135d6/1275-2026-matning-av-krok-och-virkeskvalitet-med-mobila-sensorer-pa-skordare.pdf)
- **UK Forest Research technical note** on stem straightness: visual assessment "is highly subjective and only considers a relatively small part of the saleable stem". The note compares visual, photogrammetric and terrestrial-LiDAR approaches. [GREY][SNIPPET] — [Forest Research](https://www.forestresearch.gov.uk/research/assessing-the-stem-straightness-of-trees)
- Sweep is traditionally defined as **maximum sagitta per metre of stem**. [PR] — [Prendes et al. 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4907/13/3/431)

### Inferences
- If cruiser codes are the training labels, their noise caps achievable "accuracy". Prendes et al. (2022) show visual classes 2 and 3 are statistically indistinguishable in measured sagitta, and PNC464 shows wobble is missed most of the time. Expect a ceiling well below 100% agreement even for a geometrically perfect classifier.
- Calibration therefore matters. The aim should be thresholds (SED/n over 4/6/8–12 m) that reproduce **grade-acceptance decisions and log-mix outcomes**, rather than maximum agreement with individual cruiser calls. The PlotSafe demerit matrix and harvester/mill outcomes are better targets than raw code agreement.
- Accuracy should be reported by height band (0–12, 12–20, >20 m), matching the PlotSafe audit bands. Detection and geometry degrade with height for both MLS and human VR assessment.

### Gaps
- No Australian or NZ industry trial (Interpine, Scion/FGR, HQP, FCNSW, OneFortyOne) with published confusion matrices or kappa for **automated sweep codes against cruiser codes** was found.
- No accuracy data found for the specific HQP code set (8/L/S/3/1/X/W/K), or for hybrid or Caribbean pine stem form from LiDAR.
- Scion/Hartley MLS work lists sweep as a measured trait, but no sweep-accuracy numbers were found in the accessible abstract or presentation snippets.

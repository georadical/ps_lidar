# Stem straightness in pine breeding programs: visual scoring systems and LiDAR/photogrammetry phenotyping

Scope: (a) visual straightness scoring in subtropical and temperate pine breeding programs (Queensland hybrid pine PEE × PCH, slash, Caribbean and radiata pine in Australia/NZ, plus reference programs elsewhere): scales, definitions, heritability, correlations with growth, links to measured sweep and recovery; (b) LiDAR/photogrammetry phenotyping of straightness, sweep and stem form in breeding trials; (c) public datasets. Research done November 2026; publication dates are given for each source. "Snippet-only" means the figure came from a search-engine summary and the primary text was not opened.

## 1. Visual straightness scoring scales used by breeding programs (definitions and what they measure)

### Takeaway
Australian programs (Queensland DPI/DAF hybrid pine and Caribbean pine, and STBA/Treeplan radiata) score straightness on a **1–6 relative (within-trial) scale**: 6 = straightest, and in STBA practice 6 is the straightest ~5% of stems in a trial. NZ RPBC uses a **1–9 subjective scale** (1 = crooked, 9 = very straight). Zimbabwe hybrid trials used 1–7, and UK Sitka spruce uses a 7-point scale defined geometrically by straight log lengths in the butt 6 m. Spanish *P. pinaster* breeding uses 3 classes defined by deviation relative to DBH. Only the UK and Spanish scales are tied to absolute geometry; the Australian ones are relative rankings within a site.

### Cited Findings
**Queensland (DPI/QFRI/DAF, now with HQPlantations): hybrid pine and Caribbean pine**
- In the PEE × PCH F1 hybrid family trial series (Exp. 749 TBS, 5 sites in Queensland), "stem straightness (1 = poorest tree on site, 6 = straightest tree on site)" was assessed at ages 4 and 6, together with presence/absence of double leaders and ramicorn branches. The report notes that mean straightness was "very similar across all sites, as a consequence of the relative scale used in the assessment". Site means at age 6 were 3.30–3.62, with about 2,560–3,950 trees per site. — [FWPA PNC057-0809, "Managing subtropical exotic pine plantations for improved wood production", Aug 2010](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/2573/1/PNC057-0809.pdf)
- In the Series IV hybrid pine clonal trial series (Exp. 89 PPG: about 2,000 clones, about 125 clones per family, 7 Queensland sites plus northern NSW sites), all trees were assessed at age 4 for stem straightness (1–6), double leaders, ramicorns, branch angle (1–9) and branch diameter (mm). Wind-firmness and foxtail codes came from field comments. Site mean straightness was 3.3–4.3. Parents for these families were chosen on breeding values for **volume and stem straightness** estimated from more than 85,000 trees of hybrid progeny-test data. — [FWPA PNC057-0809 (2010)](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/2573/1/PNC057-0809.pdf)
- Dieters & Nikles (1997) present Queensland results as "Volume and stem straightness (1-6, 6 good) at 9 years" for slash pine (PEE), PCH and their F1, F2 and backcross hybrids in south-east Queensland (lat. 26°S). They report that the hybrids show "improved wind-firmness, stem straightness and wood density when compared to P. caribaea", and that PCH × var. *caribaea* hybrids in central Queensland show "very good stem straightness". — [Dieters & Nikles, "The genetic improvement of Caribbean pine — building on a firm foundation", Southern Forest Tree Improvement Conf. 1997](https://rngr.net/publications/tree-improvement-proceedings/southern/1997/the-genetic-improvement-of-carribbean-pine-pinus-caribbaea-morelet-building-on-a-firm-foundation/at_download/file)
- In Queensland-bred PCH × *P. oocarpa* and PCH × *P. tecunumanii* hybrid trials, straightness in Queensland was assessed following Gwaze et al. (2000), and in Zimbabwe "on a 1 (crooked) to 7 (straight) scale", at age 5. — [Dungey, Dieters, Gwaze, Toon & Nikles, "Interspecific pine hybrids II. Genotype by environment interactions across Australia, Swaziland and Zimbabwe", Forest Genetics (received 1998, published c. 2000)](https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/9536.pdf)

**Australia: STBA / Treeplan / Tree Breeding Australia (radiata pine)**
- STBA defines early selection-criterion traits as follows: "stem straightness (STS – measured by score 1-6, 6 is the best with 5% straightest stems in a trial)". Branch size and branch angle are also scored 1–6, and branch cluster frequency is counted as the number of whorls between 1 and 6 m. The **breeding-objective** trait is instead "stem sweep (SWE – maximum deviation of log axis from straight line over the log length in mm/m)". — [FWPA PN01.1904, "Breeding Radiata Pine to Maximise Profit from Solid Wood Production: Summary Report" (2005)](https://fwpa.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2005/08/PN01.1904.pdf)
- In eight second-generation STBA radiata trials (5 mainland and 3 Tasmania), "Stem straightness was scored on a scale of 1 to 6 with 1 = most crooked stems, and 6 = straightest stems". Branch angle and branch size were scored 1–6 in the same way. Trial means were about 3.16–3.68 (SD 0.77–1.21), with 2,047–4,936 trees per trial. — [FWPA PNC050-0304, "Quantitative and molecular genetics of juvenile wood traits in radiata and slash/Caribbean pines", July 2009, Appendix 5](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/1897/1/PNC050-0304_Juvenile_Wood_Initiative_Research_Report_0.pdf)
- Tree Breeding Australia publishes radiata EBVs with "Tree straightness (SWEEP) in millimetres per metre of stem" against a national baseline of 10.5 mm/m (lower is better; for example, an EBV of −1.21 means 9.29 mm/m). It publishes BRANCH in cm (baseline 5.5 cm). Both feed an NPV index ($/ha at an 8% discount rate) together with MAI and STIFFNESS. — [Tree Breeding Australia, Radiata pine breeding values (web page, accessed Nov 2026)](https://treebreeding.com/breeding-values/radiata-pine-breeding-values)

**New Zealand: RPBC (formerly NZ Radiata Pine Breeding Cooperative)**
- Straightness is "scored on a 9-point subjective scale" (1 = crooked, 9 = very straight), at age 7–8. Branch-cluster frequency is also on a 9-point scale (1 = uninodal, 9 = extremely multinodal). The study had 5,289 (POP2) and 6,851 (POP3) straightness records. — [Li, Klápště, Telfer et al., BMC Genomics 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6935163/)
- Older NZ trials also used 1–9 for straightness ("1 = v. crooked, 9 = v. straight"). Malformation scales varied between trials (one used 1 = multileadered to 9 = no malformation; another used a 1–6 stem malformation score with 1 = multiple forks). Snippet-only; it is unclear which of the listed papers each definition comes from. — [Silvae Genetica 47 (1998) PDF](https://www.thuenen.de/media/institute/fg/PDF/Silvae_Genetica/1998/Vol._47_Heft_2-3/47_2-3_80.pdf); [NZJFS 217](https://nzjforestryscience.nz/index.php/nzjfs/article/download/217/82/3560)

**Argentina / Zimbabwe (PEE × PCH and other Queensland-derived hybrids)**
- INTA Argentina evaluated straightness, branch diameter and branch angle in PEE × PCH F1 hybrids: 5 progeny trials, 133 full-sib families, ages 3, 5 and 7. The scale definition is in the full text, which was not accessed. — [Belaber, Gauchat, Reis, Borralho & Cappa, Forest Science 64(6):595–608 (2018)](https://academic.oup.com/forestscience/article-pdf/64/6/595/26658813/fxy021.pdf)

**Reference scales outside the pine programs (useful because they are geometric)**
- UK Forestry Commission (Sitka spruce, also used for other conifers) uses a 7-point scale based on straight log lengths in the bottom 6 m: 1 = no straight length ≥2 m; 2 = one length 2–3 m; 3 = two lengths 2–3 m; 4 = one length 3–4 m; 5 = one 2–3 m plus one 3–4 m; 6 = one 4–5 m; 7 = one ≥5 m. A log is "straight" if bow does not exceed 1 cm per 1 m of length, in one plane and one direction only. Bow is the maximum deviation of the actual centre line from the straight line joining the end centres. — [MacDonald, Mochan & Connolly, Forestry 82(4):419–429 (2009)](https://academic.oup.com/forestry/article/82/4/419/549648); [Price, Hapca, Gardiner, Macdonald & Mclean, Forestry Commission Technical Note FCTN021 (2017)](https://cdn.forestresearch.gov.uk/2022/04/Assessing-the-stem-straightness-of-trees.pdf)
- FC stand grading from per-tree scores: A = >40% of trees score 6–7; B = >50% score 4–7 but <40% score 6–7; C = >35% score 3–7 but <50% score 4–7; D = <35% score 3–7 but <50% score 1; E = as D but >50% score 1. — [Price et al., FCTN021 (2017)](https://cdn.forestresearch.gov.uk/2022/04/Assessing-the-stem-straightness-of-trees.pdf)
- The Spanish *P. pinaster* breeding trial used 3 straightness classes: 1 = straight; 2 = "curved in the intermediate or basal zone and less than twice the dbh"; 3 = curved "more than twice the dbh". It used 3 lean classes: not appreciable; ≤15°; >15°. — [Prendes, Canga, Ordóñez, Majada, Acuna & Cabo, Forests 13(3):431 (2022)](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/forests/forests-13-00431/article_deploy/forests-13-00431.pdf)
- Miller (1975) is cited as testing a four-class visual scale for radiata pine, based on sweep in the lower 9–12 m of the stem, which "gave a good indication of log quality and yield of sawn timber". Snippet-only; this is a secondary citation, most likely from the MacDonald et al. (2009) introduction. — [MacDonald et al. 2009](https://academic.oup.com/forestry/article/82/4/419/549648)
- In US loblolly pine, a relative straightness scoring system was compared with a measured sweep trait and was found at least as heritable as the sweep measurement. Snippet-only. — [Williams & Lambeth, "Bole straightness measurement for advanced-generation loblolly pine genetics tests", Silvae Genetica (1989)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Bole-straightness-measurement-for-loblolly-pine-Williams-Lambeth/21d0f2d27a6191f4b65cfdb9a51610b9788e333c)
- In loblolly pine progeny trials, "visual grading is currently used" for stem straightness, branch angle and branch diameter. This motivated a stereo-vision plus deep-learning phenotyping pipeline. — [Phenotyping of Pine Tree Architecture with Stereo Vision and Deep Learning (ResearchGate, c. 2021)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353105826_Phenotyping_of_Pine_Tree_Architecture_with_Stereo_Vision_and_Deep_Learning)

### Inferences
- The Queensland hybrid and STBA radiata straightness scores are **relative, within-trial rankings** (1 = poorest on site; 6 = top ~5%). A score of 4 at one site does not equal a score of 4 at another in mm/m. Breeding scores therefore cannot be reused as absolute sweep labels for an HQP/Interpine-style classifier. They are suitable for **rank-based validation** (Spearman or Kendall correlation of a LiDAR sweep metric with the score within each trial), or for calibrating a per-site mapping.
- The scales whose definitions can be computed directly from a LiDAR centreline are:
  - the FC rule "bow ≤ 1 cm per m, one plane, one direction" and the 7-point straight-log-length score in the butt 6 m;
  - the *P. pinaster* deviation-relative-to-DBH classes, which are conceptually the same as HQP/Interpine amplitude thresholds expressed as a fraction of SED;
  - STBA/TBA SWEEP (maximum deviation of log axis from the chord, in mm/m over log length).
- The "one plane, one direction" condition in the FC rule and the double-bend logic of cruising codes (e.g. S vs single-sweep codes) need the 3-D centreline to be split into planar components and curvature sign changes to be counted. Scalar sagitta alone does not capture them.

### Gaps
- No primary document was found that gives the **exact written definitions of each score 1–6** used by Queensland DPI/DAF for hybrid pine; the only wording found is "1 = poorest tree on site, 6 = straightest tree on site". The current (post-2010) DAF/HQP protocol was not found, nor whether HQP now measures sweep in mm/m in trials.
- D.P. Kain's 2003 ANU PhD thesis on PEE × PCH genetic parameters and Powell & Nikles (1996, Silvae Genetica) were not accessible. Their straightness parameter values are unverified.
- Camcore and South African/Brazilian scales for *P. caribaea*, *P. elliottii* and hybrids, and the CFGRP (Florida) slash pine and NCSU loblolly straightness scales, were not found in primary form during this pass.
- The NZ RPBC written scoring protocol (verbal anchors for 1–9) was not found.

## 2. Heritability and genetic correlations of visual straightness scores

### Takeaway
Visual straightness is low-to-moderately heritable: individual-tree narrow-sense h² is typically about 0.1–0.3 in radiata and other hybrids, but it reaches 0.22–0.52 in Queensland PEE × PCH family trials. Clonal broad-sense H² in Queensland hybrid clones is 0.32–0.46. Across sites it is stable (type-B r ≈ 0.75–0.89). Its genetic correlation with growth is near zero in radiata and slightly unfavourable in Queensland hybrid clones.

### Cited Findings
**Queensland PEE × PCH hybrid pine**
- In Exp. 749 TBS (F1 families, 5 sites), single-site narrow-sense h² for straightness (1–6) was 0.22–0.33 at age 4 and 0.29–0.52 at age 6, with dominance proportion d² of 0.06–0.14. Growth traits had h² of about 0.07–0.30. The report summarises: "for stem straightness heritability was slightly higher (20 to 30%) and proportion of dominance was relatively small (< 10%)". The across-site type-B additive correlation for straightness at age 6 was rB(A) = 0.89 ± 0.03, against 0.43–0.56 for diameter and volume, and the dominance rB was 0.56 ± 0.10. — [FWPA PNC057-0809 (2010), Tables 24–25](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/2573/1/PNC057-0809.pdf)
- In the Series IV hybrid clones, broad-sense H² for straightness at age 4 was 0.32–0.46 across 6 sites, with type-B rB(G) = 0.84. Correlations among clone BLUPs across 7 Queensland sites:
  - straightness vs volume at age 4: −0.18
  - straightness vs height at age 4: −0.27
  - straightness vs branch diameter: −0.33
  - straightness vs ramicorns: −0.36
  - straightness vs double leaders: −0.28
  - straightness vs acoustic velocity²: −0.29 / −0.23

  The report states: "Stem straightness was however negatively correlated with all other traits measured". — [FWPA PNC057-0809 (2010), Tables 15–16](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/2573/1/PNC057-0809.pdf)
- F1 hybrid clones had "on average, superior growth, superior straightness, higher standing tree acoustic velocities" compared with F2 hybrid seedlings in a silviculture trial. — [FWPA PNC057-0809 (2010)](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/2573/1/PNC057-0809.pdf)
- For Queensland-bred PCH × POOC and PCH × PTEC hybrids at age 5, straightness h² was 0.11–0.24. Dominance trends were "greater for straightness than for the other growth traits". Straightness "was strongly correlated between most sites", whereas cross-country correlations for growth averaged only about 0.38–0.41. — [Dungey et al., Forest Genetics c. 2000](https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/9536.pdf)

**PEE × PCH hybrids outside Australia**
- Argentina (INTA): average h² for straightness was < 0.18 at ages 3–7. Growth–straightness genetic correlations were "not significant in general", and the average between-site additive correlation for growth and straightness was > 0.70. — [Belaber et al., Forest Science 2018](https://academic.oup.com/forestscience/article-pdf/64/6/595/26658813/fxy021.pdf)
- A Tree Genetics & Genomes 2015 study of subtropical pine F1 hybrids reported average h² of about 0.16 for stem straightness and 0.27 for growth. Snippet-only. Authors per Semantic Scholar are P. Mutete, R. Murepa and W. Gapare, which suggests Zimbabwe-based trials. One search summary attributed it to Queensland or Argentina, so the country is unverified. — [Mutete, Murepa & Gapare, Tree Genet. Genomes 2015 (paywalled)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11295-015-0926-2); [ResearchGate entry](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281586993_Genetic_parameters_in_subtropical_pine_F_1_hybrids_heritabilities_between-trait_correlations_and_genotype-by-environment_interactions)

**Radiata pine (Australia/NZ)**
- STBA 2nd-generation trials (8 sites): significant h² for straightness was 0.08–0.23 (mean 0.16). Combined-site correlations with straightness:
  - DBH: rA = −0.03 ± 0.10 ("independent traits")
  - density: rA = −0.28
  - branch size score: rA = +0.27 ± 0.10 (straighter trees had smaller branches)

  Average type-B rB for straightness was 0.75. Lower values (0.41–0.57) came mainly from Tasmanian trials, and rB within regions was 0.76 ± 0.07. — [FWPA PNC050-0304 (2009), Appendix 5](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/1897/1/PNC050-0304_Juvenile_Wood_Initiative_Research_Report_0.pdf)
- RPBC NZ (1–9 scale): ABLUP h² = 0.13 with the documented pedigree and 0.10 with the SNP-corrected pedigree; GBLUP h² = 0.09–0.18. Literature values cited are 0.11–0.17 in control-pollinated populations, 0.28 in juvenile clones, and 0.04–0.18 within trial series. Genomic EBV accuracy for straightness was r = 0.68–0.70. — [Li et al., BMC Genomics 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6935163/)
- The NZ + NSW synthesis gives a straightness score h² of about 0.19. One NZ trial at age 7.5 gave straightness h² = 0.16 ± 0.05 and malformation h² = 0.09 ± 0.03, and selection produced an 18% gain in bole straightness score (Shelbourne et al. 1986). Snippet-only. — [Jayawickrama, Silvae Genetica 50 (2001)](https://www.thuenen.de/media/institute/fg/PDF/Silvae_Genetica/2001/Vol._50_Heft_2/50_2_45.pdf); [Silvae Genetica 47 (1998)](https://www.thuenen.de/media/institute/fg/PDF/Silvae_Genetica/1998/Vol._47_Heft_2-3/47_2-3_80.pdf)
- In the STBA bio-economic project's correlation matrix, early STS (age 6/7) was given rG = 0.53 with MAI, −0.74 with MoE and −1.00 with SWE (sweep at rotation). The −1.00 is a constructed assumption, not an estimate. — [FWPA PN01.1904 (2005), Table 2](https://fwpa.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2005/08/PN01.1904.pdf)
- A broader review places mean radiata straightness h² at about 0.23. The Eldridge collection study reported 0.23–0.55 for straightness and branching. FAO gives 0.1–0.4. Snippet-only. — [Wu et al. review (SLU record)](https://research.slu.se/en/publications/breeding-for-wood-quality-and-profit-in-radiata-pine-a-review-of-/); [Eldridge collection, Tree Genet. Genomes](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11295-011-0449-4); [FAO radiata breeding chapter](https://www.fao.org/4/i3274e/i3274e06.pdf)

**Other pines**
- Scots pine (Latvia, age 38): stem straightness, branchiness and branch diameter had h² of 0.02–0.13. Snippet-only. — [Pinus sylvestris breeding study, Forests 11(1):23](https://doi.org/10.3390/f11010023)

### Inferences
- With h² often around 0.1–0.3, a large share of score variance is "environment + assessor noise". An objective LiDAR sweep metric could raise heritability if it removes assessor error. The radiata UAV-LiDAR preprint in Q4, where LiDAR height h² was 0.37, is suggestive but concerns height, not straightness.
- The high type-B correlations for straightness (0.75–0.89) mean straightness rankings transfer across sites better than growth rankings. Within-site relative scoring hides absolute site differences, which a LiDAR mm/m metric would expose.
- In Queensland hybrid clones, straightness is mildly negatively correlated with growth and branch size at the clone level. A sweep classifier used for clonal deployment decisions should therefore be validated separately within vigour classes.

### Gaps
- Kain (2003) and Powell & Nikles (1996) PEE × PCH parameter values were not verified. More recent HQP/DAF hybrid straightness estimates (2015–2026) were not found.
- No Camcore, South African or Brazilian *P. caribaea* or hybrid straightness heritabilities were retrieved.
- CFGRP slash pine straightness heritabilities were not retrieved.

## 3. Linking scores to measured sweep, log grade and recovery; objective metrics and assessor variability

### Takeaway
The breeding industry's objective target is sweep in mm/m: maximum deviation of the log axis from the chord over log length. Australian radiata averages about 10.3–10.5 mm/m, and sweep has a quantified economic weight. Published validations show trained visual assessors match a felled-log expert score exactly only about 45% of the time and within ±1 class about 78–81% of the time. Visual scores explain only about 45% of plot-level log recovery variation (Sitka spruce). No published regression mapping Australian 1–6 scores to mm/m was found.

### Cited Findings
**Objective sweep definitions and economic weights**
- STBA SWE is defined as the "maximum deviation of log axis from straight line over the log length in mm/m". The present mean is 10.3 mm/m. Economic functions (unit trait change):
  - grower: H = 291·MAI − 58·SWE − 248·BIX ($/ha)
  - sawmill: −1.19 $/m³ per mm/m SWE
  - integrated enterprise: −194 $/ha per mm/m

  A 10% reduction in SWE raised integrated NPV by 4.5% ($204/ha). Selection on the sawmill index was predicted to cut sweep by 4.40 mm/m. — [FWPA PN01.1904 (2005), Tables 1 and 3](https://fwpa.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2005/08/PN01.1904.pdf)
- The TBA SWEEP EBV is expressed in mm/m against a national baseline of 10.5 mm/m. — [Tree Breeding Australia](https://treebreeding.com/breeding-values/radiata-pine-breeding-values)
- Timber stiffness in radiata pine is affected by "stem straightness and slope of grain resulting from sawing logs with sweep". — [FWPA PNC050-0304 (2009)](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/1897/1/PNC050-0304_Juvenile_Wood_Initiative_Research_Report_0.pdf)
- Log sweep reference measurement in an FWPA report: a builder's string is run from one end of the log to the other and the maximum deviation from the straight line is recorded. Snippet-only. — [FWPA VNB459-1718 Resi final report (2020)](https://fwpa.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Resi_Final_Report_VNB459-1718.pdf)
- A Scion table groups logs as straight (<10 mm/m), moderate (11–20 mm/m) and severe (>20 mm/m). Snippet-only; the exact Scion paper was not verified (likely Todoroki 1998 or Monserud & Todoroki). — [Todoroki, NZJFS 28(2) 1998](https://www.scionresearch.com/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/59486/NZJFS2821998-TODOROKI242-253.pdf); [Monserud & Todoroki, "Recovery from simulated sawn logs with sweep" (403, not opened)](https://www.scionresearch.com/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/59160/05_Monserud_Todoroki.pdf)
- *P. pinaster* TLS work defines sweep traditionally as "maximum sagitta per meter of the stem". It adds **sinuosity**, the ratio of centreline length to end-to-end chord length (minimum 1), and **lean**, the angle of the PCA first axis of section centres from vertical. — [Prendes et al., Forests 2022](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/forests/forests-13-00431/article_deploy/forests-13-00431.pdf)

**Validation of visual scores and inter-assessor variability**
- Sitka spruce validation design: 25 trees, 3 observers × 3 methods (pole and string, purely visual, Vertex hypsometer) × 3 days. Trees were then felled and the butt 6 m scored by an industry expert. Results:
  - all methods: exact agreement 45%, within ±1 class 78%
  - purely visual: exact 44%, within ±1 class 81%
  - pole and string: exact 46%, within ±1 class 75%
  - Vertex: exact 46%, within ±1 class 76%
  - Friedman tests found observer effects in only 2 of 9 day × method combinations
  - plot-median score explained about 44% (R² = 44.3) of the variation in green log recovery
  - 60–100 trees per stand were needed to estimate the median and IQR

  The reference was an expert score, not direct bow measurement. — [MacDonald et al., Forestry 82(4) 2009](https://academic.oup.com/forestry/article/82/4/419/549648)
- FC technical note: visual assessment is fastest but "highly subjective and only considers a relatively small part of the saleable stem". It needs training, checking and refresher courses to stay consistent between surveyors. — [Price et al., FCTN021 (2017)](https://cdn.forestresearch.gov.uk/2022/04/Assessing-the-stem-straightness-of-trees.pdf)
- Visual straightness ratings "cannot effectively compare ratings across sites and over time because of rater interpretation bias". — [Measuring tree stem diameters and straightness with depth-image computer vision (ForSense; Bohrium summary page)](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/measuring-tree-stem-diameters-and-straightness-with-depth-image-computer-vision/831843756553535489-1500)
- In *Eucalyptus globulus* (age 9), image-analysis stem deviation was compared with a six-point subjective scale. Measuring stem deviation was judged too expensive for large-scale progeny trials. Snippet-only. — [Assessing genetic variation to improve stem straightness in Eucalyptus globulus, Ann. For. Sci. (2013/14)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13595-013-0277-9)

### Inferences
- For a PS_LiDAR sweep classifier, a realistic human-level benchmark is about 45% exact and about 80% within ±1 ordinal class against a felled-log reference. Reporting exact accuracy, ±1 accuracy, weighted Cohen's κ and per-class confusion would make results directly comparable to MacDonald et al. (2009).
- Since breeders' objective trait (SWE) is the maximum chord deviation per metre of log length, PS_LiDAR can output SWE (mm/m) per log segment alongside HQP codes. That makes its output compatible with TBA EBVs (baseline 10.5 mm/m) and the STBA economic weights.
- The −1.00 STS–SWE correlation in the STBA economic model is an assumption. Validating a LiDAR mm/m metric against 1–6 scores on the same trees would estimate the real correlation, which is a publishable contribution.

### Gaps
- Ivković et al. (2007) "Modelling the effects of stem sweep, branch size and wood stiffness of radiata pine on structural timber production" (Australian Forestry 70(3)) was found only as a title; the host had an expired TLS certificate, so the quantitative sweep-recovery functions were not retrieved. — [AFJ link (not accessible)](http://svc043.wic023v.server-web.com/pdf/pdf-members/afj/AFJ%202007%20v70/3/07Ivkovic.pdf)
- Monserud & Todoroki's sweep-recovery simulation (Scion) returned 403. Its numbers were not retrieved.
- No study was found that regresses Australian 1–6 or NZ 1–9 scores on measured mm/m sweep. No inter-assessor repeatability statistics were found for the Queensland hybrid or STBA scoring.

## 4. LiDAR/photogrammetry phenotyping of straightness, sweep and stem form (breeding trials and close analogues)

### Takeaway
Ground-based LiDAR (TLS or backpack/handheld SLAM) reliably measures diameters and stem curves (cm-level RMSE). However, **straightness-specific validation in pine breeding trials is still scarce**. The clearest case is *P. pinaster* (Spain, 2022), where TLS sagitta and sinuosity were computed from 20-cm stem sections and the visual 3-class field scores were found to separate trees poorly. UAV-LiDAR is used in radiata, loblolly and Scots pine trials mainly for height, volume and crown traits; it is "less useful for the characterisation of stem form". No published LiDAR straightness study on Queensland hybrid pine or Australian radiata trials was found.

### Cited Findings
**Pinus pinaster breeding trial, TLS (Spain) — closest analogue to PS_LiDAR**
- The trial was planted in 2005 with 225 families in rows of 4 trees, on steep (>60%) shrubby terrain. It had 408 trees after a 2018 genetic thinning, and 385 were processed. Stem sections were fitted every 20 cm from 0.5 to 4.9 m (23 sections over 4.4 m), and anomalous sections were flagged for operator review. Metrics:
  - **maximum sagitta** (distance of each section centre from the chord joining the end-section centres)
  - **standardised maximum sagitta SSmax**, which rescales stems with incomplete height to the 4.4 m reference via the circle-arc relation R = (S² + (L/2)²)/(2S)
  - **sinuosity**
  - **lean** via PCA

  — [Prendes et al., Forests 13:431 (2022)](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/forests/forests-13-00431/article_deploy/forests-13-00431.pdf)
- Repeatability and error propagation in the same study:
  - section-centre differences between the algorithm and operators were < 4 cm in 90% of estimates
  - SSmax differences were < 10 cm in 92% of trees, with TLS tending to give larger sagittas
  - lean differences were within ±0.5° in 95% of trees
  - between two human operators, SSmax differences were < 5 cm in 99% of trees

  — [Prendes et al. 2022](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/forests/forests-13-00431/article_deploy/forests-13-00431.pdf)
- Agreement with visual classes in the same study: the SSmax distributions of the 3 field straightness classes overlapped heavily, especially classes 2 and 3. Mean SSmax increased by 4.4 cm from class 1 to 2 and only 0.5 cm from 2 to 3. A Tukey-Kramer test found **no significant SSmax difference among the three visual classes**. For lean, only class 1 differed from the others. Mean SSmax was 10.4 cm against a mean stem radius of 8.8 cm. The authors argue that field techniques "often involve problems of misclassification". — [Prendes et al. 2022](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/forests/forests-13-00431/article_deploy/forests-13-00431.pdf)

**Radiata pine, New Zealand (Scion / Forest Growers Research)**
- On 884 trees of a mature radiata stand, the Emesent Hovermap (VLP-16, used as a backpack) was compared with a Riegl miniVUX-1 UAV LiDAR:
  - MLS DBH vs field: R² = 0.90, RMSE 3.42 cm (9.95%)
  - stem volume: R² = 0.99, RMSE 10.16%
  - canopy height, MLS vs ULS: R² = 0.94, RMSE 3.02%

  Field pre-harvest inventory recorded stem straightness and malformation per tree (PlotSafe, RAD05 cruising dictionary), but the report did **not** validate LiDAR straightness. It concluded "ULS is less useful for the characterisation of stem form". — [Hartley, Jayathunga, Massam et al., FGR report RFP-T014 "Capture and extraction of phenotypic traits from novel high-density point clouds" (Nov 2021)](https://fgr.nz/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/RFP-T014.pdf)
- The same report notes that ALS "is limited in its ability to describe stem form". It cites stem straightness algorithms (Mengesha et al. 2015) and Thies et al. (2004), "3-D reconstruction of stems for assessment of taper, sweep and lean based on laser scanning of standing trees", as prior work. — [FGR RFP-T014 (2021)](https://fgr.nz/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/RFP-T014.pdf)
- Personal laser scanning in a 3-ha, 9-year-old radiata breeding trial was assessed for crown and branch traits, validated on a small subset of trees (University of Canterbury thesis; full text returned 403, so details are unverified). — [Branching out: Assessing personal laser scanning as a tool… (UC repository)](https://ir.canterbury.ac.nz/server/api/core/bitstreams/09523541-31b7-41c6-a102-ddcefd7a5eb2/content)
- UAV-LiDAR in radiata genetic trials (11 trials, about 27,000 trees): LiDAR tree height had the highest h² (mean 0.37), and the 3-D convex-hull surface correlated with stem volume up to r = 0.87 with h² similar to DBH (0.26). Including malformed trees slightly weakened the proxies. This is a preprint, not peer-reviewed; country and affiliation were not verified; it covers volume, not straightness. — [Research Square preprint rs-10234920](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10234920/v1); [data and code, GitHub](https://github.com/JoaneElleouet/UAV-LiDAR_for_stem_volume_phenotyping_Prad)

**Loblolly pine, USA**
- TLS in a 15-year-old *P. taeda* progeny test (Weyerhaeuser, NC State cooperative): field DBH h² = 0.32 vs raw-TLS DBH h² = 0.17. Cleaned TLS reportedly reached 0.27 (snippet-only). Adjusted R² vs field was 0.56 for DBH and 0.29 for height. The authors conclude TLS is "a reliable alternative for genetic analysis". Whether straightness was analysed was not confirmed. — [Cavalheiro, Molina-Valero, Hodge, Howell & Acosta, J. Forestry Research 37:58 (Feb 2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11676-026-02007-0)
- A stereo-vision plus deep-learning pipeline targets loblolly progeny-trial traits (straightness, branch angle, branch diameter) as a low-cost alternative to visual grading. Accuracy numbers were not retrieved. — [ResearchGate 353105826](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353105826_Phenotyping_of_Pine_Tree_Architecture_with_Stereo_Vision_and_Deep_Learning)

**UK / Europe / Japan / other**
- FC TN021 compares three methods:
  - visual scoring: fastest
  - two-photo orthogonal photogrammetry: lower 10–12 m of the stem; diameter and orientation to within 7 mm with a 3-MP camera; up to about 10 min per tree of office processing; gives lean, sinuosity and lean orientation
  - TLS with tip-to-tail cylinder fitting: about 40 min field time per plot for 4 low-resolution scans; "quantitative", "true 3D"

  Its decision tree recommends TLS for pre-harvest assessments of more than 100 trees, and visual scoring for routine inventory. — [Price et al., FCTN021 (2017)](https://cdn.forestresearch.gov.uk/2022/04/Assessing-the-stem-straightness-of-trees.pdf)
- Japan (*Cryptomeria japonica* F1 progeny of plus trees): TLS quantified stem straightness with two standardised methods, evaluated against a field straightness index. Details were not retrieved. — [Hiraoka et al., J. Jpn. For. Soc. (2015), CiNii record](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390001205228540928?lang=en)
- ForSense (depth-image computer vision, 160 black walnut trees, Indiana): its straightness output was "highly correlated with straightness ratings by trained foresters", with "less than 4% difference in straightness value between runs" and sub-centimetre diameter repeatability. — [Measuring tree stem diameters and straightness with depth-image computer vision (Bohrium summary)](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/measuring-tree-stem-diameters-and-straightness-with-depth-image-computer-vision/831843756553535489-1500)
- Backpack MLS (Finland): stem-curve RMSE of about 1.2–1.7 cm in easy and medium plots, with volume error of about 10%. Snippet-only. — [Hyyppä et al., ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 161:246–262 (2020)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339608209_Accurate_derivation_of_stem_curve_and_volume_using_backpack_mobile_laser_scanning)
- Boreal benchmark of under- and above-canopy laser systems for stem curve and volume (2024); headline numbers were not retrieved. — [Remote Sensing 16(10):1721](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/16/10/1721)
- Scots pine (Sweden, 9-year-old clonal/progeny trial, 2025): drone-LiDAR height vs conventional height had phenotypic correlation ≥ 0.9, with very high genetic correlations and similar h². This covers height only. — [Ann. For. Sci. 2025, s13595-025-01283-w](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13595-025-01283-w)
- Douglas-fir (BC): ALS-derived height and crown metrics were used to generate breeding values. The authors note that measurement error lowers heritability. — [PMC9330362 (2022)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9330362/)
- Eucalyptus trials: UAV-LiDAR plus RGB were used for phenotypic traits and genetic assessment (crown and biomass, not straightness). — [ResearchGate 358427070](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358427070_Phenotypic_Traits_Extraction_and_Genetic_Characteristics_Assessment_of_Eucalyptus_Trials_Based_on_UAV-Borne_LiDAR_and_RGB_Images)

### Inferences
- PS_LiDAR's slice-wise ellipse-fit centreline is methodologically the same as Prendes et al.'s 20-cm section-centre approach. Their SSmax circle-arc standardisation, sinuosity and PCA lean are directly reusable, and so is their error-propagation bootstrap from section-centre error to shape metrics, which serves as a repeatability protocol.
- Prendes et al. found visual classes poorly separated by TLS sagitta, and MacDonald et al. found ±1-class human agreement of about 80%. Together these suggest visual labels are a noisy target. Validation should therefore combine (i) rank correlation with scores, (ii) agreement with felled or measured chord deviation where possible, and (iii) repeat-scan repeatability. Agreement with assessors alone is not enough.
- Prendes et al. analysed only the lowest 4.4 m, which was occlusion-limited. HQP/Interpine cruising sweep codes apply along merchantable logs, so validation must report the centreline completeness (height coverage) achieved per tree. The FGR study showed backpack MLS can recover stems in a mature radiata stand.
- No peer-reviewed TLS/MLS straightness phenotyping study for subtropical PEE × PCH hybrids was found. That is an evident niche for HQP/DAF trials where 1–6 scores already exist on more than 85,000 trees.

### Gaps
- Finnish and Swedish studies that phenotype straightness specifically in Norway spruce or Scots pine breeding trials with TLS/MLS were not located in this pass. Only stem-curve accuracy and height-phenotyping studies were found.
- No AU/NZ (Scion, STBA/Treeplan, UTAS, DAF) published study reporting LiDAR straightness vs 1–6 or 1–9 scores in breeding trials was found as of Nov 2026.
- Cavalheiro et al. (2026) full text, Hiraoka et al. (2015) results and the Canterbury PLS thesis were not accessible, so their straightness results (if any) are unverified.

## 5. Public datasets with straightness labels or measured sweep for validation

### Takeaway
No public pine dataset combining point clouds with visual straightness scores or measured sweep (mm/m) was found. The closest open resources are Finnish Scots pine TLS stem-shape and stem-point datasets (CC BY 4.0) and the data/code repository of the radiata UAV-LiDAR phenotyping preprint (contents unverified).

### Cited Findings
- "Detailed point cloud data on stem size and shape of Scots pine trees" (Zenodo 3701271, v1, March 2020, CC BY 4.0): TLS stem points from three southern Finland test sites, scanned with a Trimble TX5 in 2018 with eight scans per plot. It totals 11.2 GB with a ReadMe and includes UAV imagery for 27 plots. — [Zenodo 3701271](https://zenodo.org/records/3701271); [UEF mirror](https://erepo.uef.fi/handle/123456789/8055)
- A companion dataset of 230 Scots pine trees has field DBH and height plus TLS stem point clouds, intended for stem reconstruction and volume modelling. — [Zenodo 3712899](https://zenodo.org/record/3712899)
- The radiata UAV-LiDAR stem-volume phenotyping repository has `data` and `src` folders. Whether it contains straightness or malformation scores was not verified. — [GitHub JoaneElleouet/UAV-LiDAR_for_stem_volume_phenotyping_Prad](https://github.com/JoaneElleouet/UAV-LiDAR_for_stem_volume_phenotyping_Prad)
- The Scion/FGR Hovermap + miniVUX dataset (884 radiata trees with per-tree straightness and malformation from a RAD05 cruise) exists, but no public release was found. — [FGR RFP-T014 (2021)](https://fgr.nz/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/RFP-T014.pdf)

### Inferences
- For validation, the Finnish datasets can test centreline extraction and curvature repeatability, but they have no straightness labels. Straightness or sweep labels would have to be generated, for example by computing FC-style straight-log lengths or SWE from manually digitised centres, following Prendes et al.'s two-operator protocol.
- The most promising labelled sources are non-public: HQP/DAF hybrid pine trials with 1–6 scores, STBA/TBA radiata trials with scores, and Scion/FGR inventories with RAD05 straightness codes. Data-sharing agreements would be needed.

### Gaps
- Not searched exhaustively: other point-cloud benchmarks (e.g. international TLS benchmarks, tree-instance segmentation datasets) were not checked for straightness or sweep attributes. None found in this pass include straightness labels.

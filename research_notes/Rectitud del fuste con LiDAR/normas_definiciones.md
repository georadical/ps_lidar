# Stem / log straightness (sweep, crook, kink, wobble, bow, lean): definitions, standards and grading thresholds for log merchandising (priority: Australia / Queensland)

Research date: 19 Nov 2026. Researcher notes for the PS_LiDAR report writer. All quotes are short; "snippet-level" means that the claim comes from a search-engine extract that I could not open in full and should be treated as lower confidence.

---

## Q1. What do Australian log specifications, grading rules and Australian Standards say about sweep and straightness?

### Takeaway
The only public, citable Australian softwood log specification with numeric sweep rules that I could fully read is ForestrySA's Mt Lofty Ranges *Log Standard Specification* (SLSD002, Jan 2016) and its export product chart (Dec 2015). Both use the same convention: **sweep = mm of deflection from a straight edge laid along the log, accepted only as a uniform bow in one direction, limited to a fraction of SED, over the full log length**. Sawlog is SED/4, industrial sawlog SED/3, preservation SED/5, and export sawlog is SED/4 with a separate **wobble** limit of SED/6. I found no public HQPlantations, Forestry Corporation of NSW, OneFortyOne, HVP, Timberlands Pacific or Victorian plantation log specification with sweep numbers. The relevant Australian Standards (AS 3818.x, AS 2209, AS 2858 / AS 4785) are paywalled, so I could not verify their numeric limits.

### Cited Findings

**ForestrySA – Log Standard Specification, Mt Lofty Ranges (Document SLSD002, issued 15/01/2016, next review 01/01/2017; Pinus radiata)**
- MLR Sawlog (SAW): SED from 150 mm up ("run of bush ≥150mm Small End Diameter to 600mm+"). Log lengths 4.85 m and/or 6.05 m, with a +100 mm length tolerance. — [ForestrySA SLSD002](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2214)
- SAW sweep clause, verbatim: "Logs that are not straight shall only be accepted if the sweep is a uniform bow in one direction. Sweep shall be measured as millimetres of deflection from a straight edge laid alongside the log. Maximum sweep for all lengths shall not exceed SED/4." — [ForestrySA SLSD002](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2214)
- SAW shape clause (relevant to kink and abrupt form changes): "Abrupt changes in diameter are not permitted… where the diameter changes by more than 40% anywhere along the length of the log." Butt flare is limited to 100 mm, measured as deflection from a straight edge. — [ForestrySA SLSD002](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2214)
- MLR Industrial Sawlog (ISW): lengths 2.5 m or 3.7 m. The sweep wording is the same, but "The maximum sweep for all lengths shall not exceed SED/3." — [ForestrySA SLSD002](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2214)
- Preservation (posts SED 75–125 mm, rails 100–175 mm, strainers 125–200 mm; lengths 1.8–3.6 m): sweep only as a "uniform bow in one direction", and "Maximum sweep for all lengths and products shall not exceed SED/5". An abrupt diameter change of more than 20% is not permitted. — [ForestrySA SLSD002](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2214)
- Earlier search-engine summaries reported this document as "SED/3" for sawlog. That figure is the **ISW** clause. The SAW clause is SED/4 (checked against the full PDF text). — [ForestrySA SLSD002](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2214)

**ForestrySA – MLR Export Product Specifications (last review 17/12/2015)**. The column headers list Sweep and Wobble **as separate attributes**:
- AAX (SED 420+), ASW (SED 320+), KDS (SED 220–360): lengths 3.88 / 5.88 m; **Sweep SED/4; Wobble SED/6**. — [ForestrySA export spec](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2221)
- SPS (SED 170–260): **Sweep SED/2; Wobble SED/6**. — [ForestrySA export spec](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2221)
- KIG (SED 320+): **Sweep SED/2; Wobble SED/2**. — [ForestrySA export spec](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2221)
- Pulp P10 / P12 (SED 110–240 / 130–240): **Sweep SED/1.5 if SED >200 mm, SED/1 if <200 mm; Wobble SED/1**. — [ForestrySA export spec](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2221)
- General rules: "Abrupt diameter changes by more than 20% are not permitted". Roundness: longest face ≤1.25× shortest for A, K and KIG. — [ForestrySA export spec](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2221)

**Queensland (slash pine, the only Queensland log-level sweep data found)**
- FWPA project PN06.3016 (Sept 2008; DPI&F with FPQ, Weyerhaeuser and Hyne): sample stems were "cut to a standard log length of 4.8m (final product length) plus top log (between 2.4 and 4.8m)". Sweep was recorded by mill log scanners, not by hand. — [FWPA PN06.3016, Resource characterization of slash pine](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/3144/2/PN06.3016_Slash_pine_Resource_Evaluation_2009%5B1%5D-sec.pdf)
- Mean measured sweep per sawlog (4.87–5.0 m logs, SED 174–243 mm): 14–24 mm at Beerburrum and 18–19 mm at Toolara. The butt log was highest (24 mm at Beerburrum). — [FWPA PN06.3016](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/3144/2/PN06.3016_Slash_pine_Resource_Evaluation_2009%5B1%5D-sec.pdf)
- The same report notes that "the influence of sweep and taper will affect where cuts are made". Inventory cores are taken "perpendicular to any sweep or tree lean" to avoid compression wood. It also notes that Caribbean pine becomes the predominant clearfall species after 2012/13. — [FWPA PN06.3016](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/3144/2/PN06.3016_Slash_pine_Resource_Evaluation_2009%5B1%5D-sec.pdf)
- HQPlantations manages Queensland's state plantation estate under a 99-year plantation licence. — [Business Queensland, softwood plantation estate](https://www.business.qld.gov.au/industries/farms-fishing-forestry/forests-wood/plantation-forestry/state-land)
- Snippet-level: a DAF factsheet describes the southern pine estate (~148,000 ha) as producing mostly sawlog and ply log, plus some pulpwood. — [DAF Southern pine factsheet, May 2017](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/3941/4/Southern%20pine%20final%20factsheet_update%20May%202017.pdf)
- Snippet-level: KPMG's Australian Pine Log Price Index (commissioned by HVP) lists HQPlantations SE Queensland as a grower region. Its log categories (small, intermediate, medium and large sawlog; preservation; pulp; salvage) are size-based, and the extract shows no sweep criteria. — [APLPI June 2017](https://www.timberbiz.com.au/wp-content/uploads/APLPI-Jun-17-Stumpage-Public.pdf)

**Australian Standards (paywalled)**
- AS 3818.11 *Timber – Heavy structural products – Visually graded – Utility poles*: the 2009 edition has been superseded by **AS 3818.11:2026**. Retailers disagree on status. The scope is visually graded poles for overhead distribution and telecommunications lines. — [Standards Ireland shop, AS 3818.11:2026](https://shop.standards.ie/en-ie/standards/as-3818-11-2026-129696_saig_as_as_4020649/); [ABLIS AS 3818.11-2009](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/wa/as-3818-11-2009-timber-heavy-structural-products-visually-graded-utility-poles/17428)
- Snippet-level: an industry presentation lists "straightness" as an AS 3818.11 size and form criterion but gives no numbers. The AS 3818.1 glossary contains entries for both "sweep" and "crook". — [AS 3818.1-2009 store page](https://store.standards.org.au/product/as-3818-1-2009)

### Inferences
- Australian softwood practice, at least in the ForestrySA lineage, defines sweep as **one number per log over the full log length**, with **single-direction bow only**. Any multi-direction curvature (S-bend or wobble) makes a domestic log non-conforming, or in the export chart is limited separately as "wobble".
- SED-fraction limits are length-agnostic ("for all lengths"), so the allowable curvature is tighter on longer logs. For a constant-radius bow the mid-ordinate h ≈ L²/(8R) (geometry, not from a source). An SED/4 limit on a 6.05 m log is therefore about 0.64 times as permissive in curvature as the same SED/4 on a 4.85 m log.
- Worked example (my arithmetic): a 4.85 m SAW log with SED 200 mm may have ≤50 mm sweep, about 10 mm/m. That is the same order as the UK, Finnish and German rule of thumb of 1 cm/m (see Q3). So SED/4 over about 5 m is broadly equivalent to 1 cm/m for 200 mm logs, but becomes more lenient for larger SEDs.
- For Queensland pine, the typical sawlog length is about 4.8–4.9 m (slash pine study) rather than radiata's 4.85 / 6.05 m. Mean butt-log sweep of about 24 mm on SED of about 243 mm is roughly SED/10, well inside SED/4.

### Gaps
- **HQPlantations log specifications**: no public sawlog, ply/peeler, preservation or pulp spec with sweep, wobble or kink limits was found. A search snippet suggests HQP issues a "wood supply specification" to customers, but it is not public. The PS_LiDAR team should request the current HQP log spec and the LIR18-HQP dictionary definitions directly.
- **Forestry Corporation of NSW, OneFortyOne, HVP (Victoria), Timberlands Pacific (Tasmania)**: no public log spec with sweep numbers found. Several searches returned only ForestrySA material.
- **AS 3818.11 (2009/2026), AS 2209-1994, AS 3818.1 glossary, AS 2858 / AS 4785 (sawn softwood bow, spring and crook)**: numeric limits and definitions not verified (paywalled). Do not assume figures.
- ForestrySA SLSD002 is dated 2016 with a review due in 2017. A newer version may exist, and ForestrySA's role has since changed, so it may not reflect current Green Triangle / OneFortyOne practice.

## Q2. Where does the HQP / Interpine "LiDAR Feature Cruising Dictionary" (LIR18-HQP) family come from? Public information on HQP inventory and cruising standards

### Takeaway
No public copy of the LIR18-HQP dictionary or any HQP cruising standard was found. Public Interpine material shows that the sweep-coding philosophy in PS_LiDAR's code set (gun-barrel "8", "L" for longs, "S" for shorts, SED-fraction classes, separate wobble) comes from Interpine's NZ radiata cruising dictionaries (RAD05, RAD05A, RAD06, run through YTGEN). Those dictionaries descend from the NZ Forest Research MARVL / Atlas Cruiser stem-description system and its sweep classes. The SED-fraction ladder (d/8, d/4, d/3, d/2, d…) is the NZ Forest Research / MPI sweep-class scheme.

### Cited Findings
- Interpine (D. Herries, 26 Nov 2013): its cruising dictionaries **RAD05, RAD05A and RAD06** split sawlog sweep into **"Longs" and "Shorts"** codes even though "the sweep requirement is essentially the same (e.g. SED 1/4 over 6m)". The split separates stems that can only make short logs from stems that can make long logs. — [Interpine, Better predicting yield of long logs vs short logs](https://interpine.nz/better-predicting-yield-of-long-logs-vs-short-logs-of-the-same-grade-quality/)
- Same source: "Shorts" is "a gentle single sweep sawlog over a 6m length" that gains sweep in more than one direction beyond 6 m. "Longs" is "a single gentle sweep sawlog, with movement in one consistent direction". Stems acceptable for shorts may fail at 8–12 m because of "double or multiple sweep in 2 or 3 directions". — [Interpine](https://interpine.nz/better-predicting-yield-of-long-logs-vs-short-logs-of-the-same-grade-quality/)
- Same source (paraphrased): in NZ the split is usually domestic ≤6.1 m versus export lengths up to 12 m. **In Australian dictionaries the split is often ≤4.6 m versus up to 6.1 m.** — [Interpine](https://interpine.nz/better-predicting-yield-of-long-logs-vs-short-logs-of-the-same-grade-quality/)
- Same source: the example output uses sweep codes **"Sw:8,L"**. Calling "L" (longs) lets YTGEN cut logs over 6.1 m (7.4, 8.1 and 12.1 m in the example). There was about a **10% value difference** between the short and long scenarios. — [Interpine](https://interpine.nz/better-predicting-yield-of-long-logs-vs-short-logs-of-the-same-grade-quality/)
- Same source on wobble: "severe wobble, unacceptable in shorts, needs to be accurately noted in the stem description". Mild sinuosity "doesn't warrant description as wobble or even a short section of SED/3 (rough sawlogs)". — [Interpine](https://interpine.nz/better-predicting-yield-of-long-logs-vs-short-logs-of-the-same-grade-quality/)
- MARVL ("method of assessment of recoverable volume by log type") "allows codes and defects to be assigned to lengths of the bole of sampled trees". In one audit its quality-code dictionary had 14 codes, with the default code "N" overused. — [NZFFA Tree Grower, May 2005, MARVL pre-harvest inventory system](https://c.pool.nzffa.org.nz/farm-forestry-model/tree-grower-articles/may-2005/marvl-pre-harvest-inventory-system/)
- Snippet-level: NZQA unit standard 20584 (pre-harvest inventory) lists "branch size classes, branch angle, sweep, visual defects, out of round" as the stem assessment range, described per the NZ Forest Research *Atlas Cruiser User's Guide*. — [NZQA US 20584](https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/nqfdocs/units/pdf/20584.pdf)
- Snippet-level, unverified: an Interpine blog post on backpack-LiDAR inventory lists HQPlantations among its Australian and NZ forest-owner collaborators. — [Interpine LiDAR category](https://interpine.nz/category/lidar/page/3/)

### Inferences
- PS_LiDAR's codes map onto the Interpine RAD-dictionary structure:
  - "8" = straightest SED/8 class (cf. "Sw:8").
  - "L" / "S" = longs and shorts.
  - "3" = SED/3 rough sawlog.
  - "1" = SED/1, pulp-type tolerance.
  - "W" = wobble.
  - "K" = kink.
- These codes are therefore **cruising (stem-description) classes, not log-grade specifications**. They are designed so the bucking optimiser (YTGEN) can test logs of different lengths against the buyer's log specs. Anchoring them to an Australian log spec means checking that each class boundary, applied over its window, still separates the sweep tolerances of the products HQP actually cuts.
- Discrepancy to flag: Interpine describes the generic longs/shorts sweep as "SED 1/4 over 6 m", but PS_LiDAR's L and S use SED/5. The Australian split is described as 4.6 m versus 6.1 m, while PS_LiDAR uses <6.1 m versus >6.1 m. Both may be legitimate HQP customisations, but only the LIR18-HQP dictionary text can confirm them.

### Gaps
- The LIR18-HQP dictionary text, its version history and HQP's field cruising manual are not public. Window lengths (6 m, 4 m, 0.3–1 m), the W threshold (>5 cm over 4 m) and the K definition (≤0.5 m) could not be traced to any public HQP, Interpine or DAF source.
- No public description was found of HQP's operational LiDAR inventory program (TLS/MLS plots, TreeTools / SilvaCloud use).
- The MARVL / Atlas Cruiser manual with its formal sweep-code definitions was not found online (Scion-held).

## Q3. How are sweep and crook formally defined and measured internationally (NZ, Nordic, EN / European, UK, USA)?

### Takeaway
Two measurement families exist:
- **Chord-based maximum deviation (arc height or "Pfeilhöhe")**, expressed either as a fraction of SED over the whole log (NZ, Australia) or in cm per metre (Germany RVR / EN tradition, UK, Finland; Sweden's old VMR 99 used bow height as a percentage of length).
- **Sawing-cylinder / yield-loss methods**: Sweden's current VMR 07 "utbytesförlust" and US cubic scaling length and percentage deductions.

Crook or kink is everywhere treated as a **sudden, localised bend** handled as a point defect (cut out or downgraded locally), distinct from gradual sweep.

### Cited Findings

**New Zealand (MPI / Canopy; Forest Research sweep classes)**
- NEFD yield-table log types: Pruned (SED 35 cm, max branch 0, **sweep class 1**), Unpruned (SED 20 cm, max branch 14 cm, **sweep class 1**), Pulp (SED 10 cm, **sweep class 2**). — [Canopy / MPI, National exotic forest description yield tables (page updated 10 Sep 2026; data = 2015 yield tables)](https://canopy.govt.nz/forestry-data-research/national-exotic-forest-description)
- Sweep class limits by log length (d = SED). Class 1: <3.7 m **d/8**; 3.7–4.8 m **d/4**; 4.9–7.6 m **d/3**; >7.6 m **d/2**. Class 2: **d, 2d, 3d, 4d** over the same bands. — [Canopy / MPI NEFD](https://canopy.govt.nz/forestry-data-research/national-exotic-forest-description)
- Snippet-level: an older reproduction of NZ domestic grades (P1, P2, S1–S3, L1–L3, Pulp), based on a 2000 Forest Research bulletin, defines sweep as the maximum deviation from straightness along the length of the log. MPI notes that grade names vary and that specifications differ between suppliers. — [ANU-hosted NZ log grades](https://fennerschool-associated.anu.edu.au/marketreport/NZ_log_grades.pdf) (server refused the connection; snippet only); [MPI NZ log grades](https://www.mpi.govt.nz/forestry/forest-industry-and-workforce/forestry-wood-processing-data/new-zealand-log-grades-for-radiata-pine) (page did not render; snippet only)

**Sweden (Biometria / VMR)**
- VMR 1_07 sawlog instruction (Jan 2019, ver. 1). Straightness ("Rakhet") is graded by **utbytesförlust (yield loss) = "length of the sawing cylinder that, because of crook, does not fit in the log"**. The sawing cylinder is "a straight cylinder based on the top diameter under bark minus 15 mm" (minus 1 cm if diameter is measured in cm classes). The loss is assessed on one side of the log. — [VMR 1_07 sågtimmer 2019](https://www.mellanskog.se/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/matinstruktioner-2019-webb.pdf)
- Thresholds: **<20 cm loss = tolerance (no downgrade); 21–120 cm = lowest class; >120 cm = reject (vrak)**. Pine classes 1–3 and spruce class 1: max 20 cm loss. Pine class 4 and spruce class 2: max 120 cm. Docking-off is not applied. — [VMR 1_07](https://www.mellanskog.se/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/matinstruktioner-2019-webb.pdf)
- **"Tvärkrök/toppbrott" (sharp crook / top break)** is "Tillåts ej" (not allowed) in pine classes 1–3 and spruce class 1, and allowed in the lowest class. — [VMR 1_07](https://www.mellanskog.se/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/matinstruktioner-2019-webb.pdf)
- Skogforsk Arbetsrapport 1275–2026 (Lindgren et al., 2026) distinguishes **tvärkrök** (sharp, short crook) from **långkrök** (long sweep) and lists three sweep metrics. **Båghöjd (bow height)** is "the maximum orthogonal distance between the stem's actual centre line and a straight line drawn between the log's end points", computed in 3D and also expressed as a % of log length, as in the old VMR 99. **Bågradie (radius of curvature)** is derived from bow height and length (Prendes et al. 2022). **Utbytesförlust** is the measure in current rules (Biometria 2025). — [Skogforsk 1275-2026](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/382d558622a34fc5989b895e686135d6/1275-2026-matning-av-krok-och-virkeskvalitet-med-mobila-sensorer-pa-skordare.pdf)
- Same report: "the older VMR 99 (bow height in % of log length) is more permissive and generates fewer rejects", while "VMR 07 based on utbytesförlust is stricter". Bow height computes faster, and yield loss is "more sensitive to measurement uncertainty". — [Skogforsk 1275-2026](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/382d558622a34fc5989b895e686135d6/1275-2026-matning-av-krok-och-virkeskvalitet-med-mobila-sensorer-pa-skordare.pdf)
- Same report on point versus distributed defects: tvärkrök, forks and spike knots have a fixed position, so the affected zone is classed as pulp and no sawlog may be placed over it. Long sweep "depends on how the stem is cut". A log from 0–430 cm may be straight enough while 0–550 cm is too crooked, so each candidate log must be tested against the sweep metric. — [Skogforsk 1275-2026](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/382d558622a34fc5989b895e686135d6/1275-2026-matning-av-krok-och-virkeskvalitet-med-mobila-sensorer-pa-skordare.pdf)
- Same report, LiDAR performance: harvester-mounted MLS bow height at 1–5 m above ground had **RMSE 9.1 mm, bias −5.7 mm** (wide-angle scanner) against TLS. Mean bow height was about 17 mm, so "the average tree is straight in the first log". For Scots pine, sweep is the most common reason Swedish sawmills downgrade or reject logs. — [Skogforsk 1275-2026](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/382d558622a34fc5989b895e686135d6/1275-2026-matning-av-krok-och-virkeskvalitet-med-mobila-sensorer-pa-skordare.pdf)
- Snippet-level (review paper, PDF returned 403): "In the Swedish log grading system (1999) logs with a bow height higher than 1% are downgraded". Finland has no general grading rule defining an overall bow-height limit. Timell (1986) is cited for crook = abrupt bend, sweep = gradual bend, bow = gradual bend deviating in two directions. — [core.ac.uk 401691783](https://core.ac.uk/download/401691783.pdf)

**Finland (buyer trade terms, not a national standard; snippet-level)**
- Sawlogs: "lenkoutta enintään 1 cm/juoksumetri" (bow max 1 cm per running metre). Pine/spruce veneer logs: 1.5 cm/m. Pine pulpwood: bow ≤5 cm over 1 m; "jyrkkää mutkaa ja monivääryyttä ei sallita" (sharp bends and multiple crookedness not allowed). — Search snippet, source likely a municipal timber-sale document ([ruovesi.oncloudos.com](https://ruovesi.oncloudos.com/kokous/2023177-4-4809.PDF)); unverified.

**Germany (RVR, 3rd ed. 1 Jul 2020) – the clearest written single versus multiple sweep rule**
- *Einfache Krümmung* (single sweep) = deviation of the log's long axis from a straight line "characterised by only one bend in one plane". *Mehrfache Krümmung* (multiple sweep) = "at least two bends in one or more planes". — [RVR brochure (FNR)](https://www.fnr.de/fileadmin/Projekte/2023/RVR_Medien/P23607_Brosch_RVR_Nachdruck_210x148mm_v05r03_231130_web.pdf)
- Measurement: the maximum distance in cm (Pfeilhöhe P) between the concave surface and a straight line joining the innermost points of the end faces, divided by length L in m, **reported in cm/m**. **For butt logs, measurement starts 1 m above the stem foot.** For multiple sweep the log is notionally cut "in the region of the inflection point" into single-sweep sections (L1, L2), each assessed separately under the single-sweep rule. — [RVR brochure](https://www.fnr.de/fileadmin/Projekte/2023/RVR_Medien/P23607_Brosch_RVR_Nachdruck_210x148mm_v05r03_231130_web.pdf)
- Limits (spruce/fir, Annex III-a): class A ≤1.0 cm/m. Class B ≤1.0 cm/m (<35 cm diameter) and ≤1.5 cm/m (≥35 cm). Pine (Annex III-b), class shown: ≤1.0 cm/m. Oak/beech tables: ≤2 and ≤3 cm/m. — [RVR brochure](https://www.fnr.de/fileadmin/Projekte/2023/RVR_Medien/P23607_Brosch_RVR_Nachdruck_210x148mm_v05r03_231130_web.pdf)
- Snippet-level: Mercer Holz sawlog criteria for Pinus sylvestris give "single-stringed curvature: 1a-1b ≤ 1.0 cm/m; 2a-3a ≤ 1.0 cm/m; 3b+ ≤ 1.5 cm/m". — [Mercer Holz eligibility criteria (2021)](https://de.mercerint.com/wp-content/uploads/MH-Eligibility-criteria-for-sawable-logs-EN-20210318.pdf)

**European Standard EN 1309-3:2018**
- EN 1309-3:2018 *Round and sawn timber – Methods of measurements – Part 3: Features and biological degradations* **supersedes EN 1310:1997 and EN 1311:1997**. Sweep is clause **6.1.1** under "6.1 Features of the form of the log", alongside taper (6.1.2), ovality (6.1.3) and rate of growth (6.1.4). — [DIN EN 1309-3 table of contents](https://www.din.de/de/mitwirken/normenausschuesse/nhm/wdc-beuth:din21:291166135/toc-2881645/download); [iTeh EN 1309-3:2018](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/cen/800dfb80-b50b-4f0a-8e8d-258724935b59/en-1309-3-2018)
- Snippet-level: the standard is for appearance grading, not structural strength grading (EN 14081-1), and "the methodology of measurement used shall be stated". — [NSAI I.S. EN 1309-3:2018](https://shop.standards.ie/en-ie/standards/i-s-en-1309-3-2018-881026_saig_nsai_nsai_2092918)

**UK (Forestry Commission, archived)**
- Information Note, Nov 1998 (Methley), adopting *Field Book 9* (FC 1993): "Bow not to exceed 1 cm for every 1 m length and this in one plane and one direction only." Bow is measured as "the maximum deviation at any point of a straight line joining centres at each end of the log from the actual centre line of the log". A log with bow in more than one direction is not straight. — [FC Information Note, Stem straightness pilot study](https://cdn.forestresearch.gov.uk/1998/01/fcin010.pdf)
- Standing-tree assessment was restricted to the **first 6 m butt section**. It counts straight log lengths in four classes: ≥4 m (structural / carcassing), 3–4 m, 2–3 m (fencing, pallet) and <2 m (industrial). — [FC IN 1998](https://cdn.forestresearch.gov.uk/1998/01/fcin010.pdf)

**USA**
- USFS PNW Research Note PNW-RN-479 (Cahill & Cegelka, 1989): "crook is a sudden curve or bend in the log; sweep is a gradual curve in the log". Crook and sweep are handled by **length deductions** in cubic scaling. — [PNW-RN-479](https://www.fs.usda.gov/pnw/pubs/pnw_rn479.pdf)
- New Hampshire best scaling practices guide (based on the USFS Log Scaling Handbook): "Sweep or sidebend requires a scale deduction". The deduction is (maximum departure − allowance) / diameter, as a % of gross scale, with an allowance of **2 in for 16 ft logs and 1 in for 8 ft logs**. Worked example: 6 in departure on a 14 in log gives 29%. Crook is "severe sidebend". The guide includes deduction tables by log length (10, 12 and 16 ft), diameter and sweep. — [NH Best Log Scaling Practices Guide](https://extension.unh.edu/sites/default/files/migrated_unmanaged_files/Resource000255_Rep277.pdf)
- Snippet-level: the current FSH 2409.11 revision "adds new direction and exhibits for crook defect deduction process and adds clarification of scaling cylinder position when deducting for sweep". FSH 2409.11a has an exhibit on "length measurement of log with sweep or crook". — [FSH 2409.11a ch. 20](https://www.usda.gov/sites/default/files/guidance-documents/ForestService.Handbook%202409.11a%2020%20Cubic%20Log%20Scaling%20Rules%2022.3-22.41.pdf) (HTTP 403; snippet only)

**Tropical pines / breeding (relevant to PHYB, slash and Caribbean pine)**
- Barnes & Gibson (1986), "A method to assess stem straightness in tropical pines", *Commonwealth Forestry Review* 65:168–171. Snippet-level: a Queensland slash pine genetics study applied it by scoring each of **six 1-m sections of the butt log** and then summing or weighting the scores. — [UK gov R4D record](https://www.gov.uk/research-for-development-outputs/a-method-to-assess-stem-straightness-in-tropical-pines)

### Inferences
- Across jurisdictions the **core geometric primitive is the same**: the maximum perpendicular distance between the actual stem axis (or surface) and the chord joining the log-end centres (or end-surface points). What changes is the normaliser:
  - SED fraction (NZ, Australia): diameter-relative, like a sawing-cylinder rule.
  - Length (cm/m: UK, RVR, Finland; % of length: VMR 99): curvature-like.
  - Sawing-cylinder misfit length (VMR 07): combines diameter and curvature.
- For a LiDAR pipeline that already reconstructs the stem centreline, implementing all three normalisations is cheap. Bow height h per candidate log window, h/SED and h/L can all be derived, and a VMR-07-style straight-cylinder fit gives yield loss directly.
- The SED/k rule is approximately a sawing-cylinder rule. A log with single sweep h ≤ SED/k still contains a straight cylinder of diameter about SED·(1 − 1/k) along its length (geometric reasoning, ignoring taper). SED/4 then keeps about a 75% SED straight core, SED/1 keeps essentially none, and SED/8 keeps about 87.5%. This explains why NZ, ForestrySA and Interpine use SED fractions for sawlogs.
- Surface-based measurement (a straight edge along the log, as in ForestrySA and RVR "concave surface") and centreline-based measurement (UK, Skogforsk 3D) give nearly identical h for a single bow. They diverge with ovality or bumps, so for LiDAR the centreline of fitted circles is the defensible choice and is what Skogforsk used.
- **Window choice matters quadratically.** For a constant-curvature bow, h scales with L², so the same stem curvature gives 0.44× the deviation over 4 m as over 6 m. "SED/5 over 6 m" is therefore roughly "SED/11 over 4 m" for a uniform arc. PS_LiDAR's mixed windows (6 m for 8/L, 4 m for S/3/W) are not directly comparable unless converted.

### Gaps
- EN 1309-3:2018 clause 6.1.1 text (whether it uses cm/m, chord length, butt-offset or single/multiple rules) was not accessible; it is paywalled. The RVR definitions above are likely aligned with it, but that is unverified.
- StanForD 2010: no public field definition for crook or bow was found. StanForD is a data-exchange standard, and quality limits come from buyer instructions or Biometria.
- The Finnish national position was found only at snippet level.
- The USFS sweep-deduction text (current FSH 2409.11a) could not be fetched (HTTP 403).
- The NHLA / USFS hardwood log grade sweep allowances (often quoted as % of gross volume per grade) were not verified.

## Q4. Single versus multiple (compound) sweep, crook, kink, lean and wobble; choice of measurement windows; sweep relative to SED and log length

### Takeaway
Authoritative sources agree on the following:
- **Sweep** is a gradual curve. It is acceptable only in one plane and one direction for sawlogs (Australia, UK, RVR single sweep).
- **Multiple or compound sweep** (S-bends, two or more planes) is either rejected, split at the inflection point and assessed per section (RVR), or coded separately as **wobble** or sinuosity (ForestrySA export chart, Interpine).
- **Crook or kink** is a sudden, localised bend, treated as a point defect. The zone is cut out or downgraded (Swedish tvärkrök, USFS crook, Skogforsk bucking).
- **Lean** is a whole-tree inclination. It is not a log-grade attribute, but it matters for compression wood and for inventory core orientation.

Windows are driven by the **product log lengths** being merchandised: 4.8–6.1 m sawlogs in Australia, the 6 m butt log in UK standing-tree assessment, 1 m sections in tropical-pine breeding scores, and every candidate log in optimised bucking.

### Cited Findings
- Single direction only: ForestrySA accepts non-straight logs "only… if the sweep is a uniform bow in one direction". — [ForestrySA SLSD002](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2214)
- UK FC: bow must be "in one plane and one direction only". A log with bow in more than one direction fails. — [FC IN 1998](https://cdn.forestresearch.gov.uk/1998/01/fcin010.pdf)
- RVR: multiple curvature has "at least two bends in one or more planes". The log is split at the inflection point into single-sweep sections, each assessed separately. Butt logs are measured from 1 m above the stem foot, which excludes butt flare. — [RVR brochure](https://www.fnr.de/fileadmin/Projekte/2023/RVR_Medien/P23607_Brosch_RVR_Nachdruck_210x148mm_v05r03_231130_web.pdf)
- Wobble as a separate attribute with its own SED-fraction limit, tighter than sweep for export sawlogs: Sweep SED/4 and Wobble SED/6 (AAX, ASW, KDS). Pulp: Wobble SED/1. — [ForestrySA export spec](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2221)
- Wobble or sinuosity in cruising: severe wobble must be described. Mild sinuosity is not coded as wobble "or even a short section of SED/3". — [Interpine](https://interpine.nz/better-predicting-yield-of-long-logs-vs-short-logs-of-the-same-grade-quality/)
- Crook versus sweep: "crook is a sudden curve or bend… sweep is a gradual curve". — [PNW-RN-479](https://www.fs.usda.gov/pnw/pubs/pnw_rn479.pdf). Crook = "severe sidebend". — [NH guide](https://extension.unh.edu/sites/default/files/migrated_unmanaged_files/Resource000255_Rep277.pdf)
- Sharp crook as a point defect: the Swedish tvärkrök is not allowed in the higher classes. — [VMR 1_07](https://www.mellanskog.se/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/matinstruktioner-2019-webb.pdf). In automated bucking the tvärkrök zone is classed as pulp so no sawlog can span it. — [Skogforsk 1275-2026](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/382d558622a34fc5989b895e686135d6/1275-2026-matning-av-krok-och-virkeskvalitet-med-mobila-sensorer-pa-skordare.pdf)
- Abrupt form change as a defect in the Australian spec (kink-adjacent): diameter changes over 40% (sawlog) or 20% (preservation, export) are not permitted. — [ForestrySA SLSD002](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2214); [ForestrySA export spec](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2221)
- Lean: in Queensland slash pine work, cores are taken "perpendicular to any sweep or tree lean" to avoid compression wood. — [FWPA PN06.3016](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/3144/2/PN06.3016_Slash_pine_Resource_Evaluation_2009%5B1%5D-sec.pdf). Snippet-level: Scion notes that severe compression wood is "normally caused by tree lean or extreme stem wobble". — [Scion NZJFS Turner 1999](https://www.scionresearch.com/__data/assets/pdf_file/0007/59461/NZJFS291_1999_146_164TURNER.pdf)
- Bow terminology conflict: UK FC uses "bow" for single-plane, single-direction deviation. Timell (1986), as cited in a review (snippet-level), uses "bow" for a gradual bend deviating in two directions. — [FC IN 1998](https://cdn.forestresearch.gov.uk/1998/01/fcin010.pdf); [core.ac.uk review](https://core.ac.uk/download/401691783.pdf)
- Window by log length:
  - NZ sweep classes vary the allowed SED fraction by length band: <3.7 m d/8 up to >7.6 m d/2 in class 1. — [Canopy / MPI](https://canopy.govt.nz/forestry-data-research/national-exotic-forest-description)
  - ForestrySA applies one SED fraction "for all lengths". — [ForestrySA SLSD002](https://www.forestrysa.com.au/?p=2214)
  - Skogforsk evaluates sweep for every candidate log interval (0–430 cm versus 0–550 cm). — [Skogforsk 1275-2026](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/382d558622a34fc5989b895e686135d6/1275-2026-matning-av-krok-och-virkeskvalitet-med-mobila-sensorer-pa-skordare.pdf)
  - Researchers prefer maximum bow height over mm/m when bottom-log lengths vary (snippet-level). — [core.ac.uk review](https://core.ac.uk/download/401691783.pdf)
- Butt-log window in standing-tree assessment: the UK uses the first 6 m. — [FC IN 1998](https://cdn.forestresearch.gov.uk/1998/01/fcin010.pdf). Barnes & Gibson use six 1-m sections of the butt log (snippet-level). — [gov.uk R4D](https://www.gov.uk/research-for-development-outputs/a-method-to-assess-stem-straightness-in-tropical-pines)

### Inferences
- Defensible operational definitions for PS_LiDAR, anchored to the sources above:
  - **Sweep (single)**: maximum orthogonal distance of the fitted centreline from the chord between window end-centres, with a one-sided deviation sign along a single dominant plane.
  - **Compound sweep / wobble**: the centreline crosses its chord, meaning one or more inflection points in the window. Following RVR, either split at inflection points and evaluate sections, or code as wobble, as the ForestrySA export chart does with its own (tighter) SED/6 limit.
  - **Kink / crook**: a localised direction change whose deviation is concentrated over a short length (sub-metre). Treat it as a point defect, as with the Swedish tvärkrök.
- PS_LiDAR's W threshold (>5 cm over 4 m) is absolute rather than SED-relative. ForestrySA's export wobble limit is SED/6 (≈33–50 mm for SED 200–300 mm), so the two are of similar magnitude for typical Queensland sawlog SEDs (about 175–245 mm in PN06.3016). An SED-relative wobble threshold would match the Australian precedent better.
- Window lengths should mirror HQP's actual cut lengths (Queensland slash pine about 4.8–4.9 m in the published study, and possibly 6.1 m longs), not NZ export lengths (up to 12 m). This is the main jurisdictional fix.
- Exclude the first ~1 m (RVR butt rule) or the butt-flare zone when computing sweep on butt logs, so butt swell and root flare are not counted as sweep.

### Gaps
- No Australian source formally defines "kink", "lean" or "wobble" with a measurement method. Only ForestrySA's export chart gives wobble an SED-fraction limit, without a measurement window.
- No source was found that justifies the specific 0.3–1 m (X), ≤0.5 m (K) or 4 m (S, 3, W) windows used in PS_LiDAR.

## Q5. Published conversions between field cruising sweep codes and sawlog / peeler / pulp outturn in Australian pine

### Takeaway
I found **no published Australian conversion** from cruising sweep codes to sawlog, peeler or pulp outturn for Queensland exotic pines. The available evidence is indirect: optimiser-based value differences, buyer specs that set the product breakpoints, and overseas deduction tables.

### Cited Findings
- Interpine: two otherwise identical trees coded longs versus shorts differed by about **10% in value** in YTGEN. — [Interpine](https://interpine.nz/better-predicting-yield-of-long-logs-vs-short-logs-of-the-same-grade-quality/)
- Skogforsk 2026: crook-aware bucking on TLS stem geometry raised one example tree's value from **SEK 112.8 to 156.5** and increased the sawlog share. The choice of rule set (VMR 99 versus VMR 07) changes reject rates. — [Skogforsk 1275-2026](https://www.skogforsk.se/contentassets/382d558622a34fc5989b895e686135d6/1275-2026-matning-av-krok-och-virkeskvalitet-med-mobila-sensorer-pa-skordare.pdf)
- USA: sweep deduction tables give % of gross scale. For example, a 10 ft log of 14 in diameter with 6 in sweep has a **30%** deduction. — [NH guide](https://extension.unh.edu/sites/default/files/migrated_unmanaged_files/Resource000255_Rep277.pdf). Crook caused relatively small absolute lumber loss (−0.9 ft³) but a high percentage loss in small logs. — [PNW-RN-479](https://www.fs.usda.gov/pnw/pubs/pnw_rn479.pdf)
- Snippet-level: an ANU thesis on radiata pine (Benyon) derived economic weights for straightness by regressing value-recovery loss due to sweep on subjective straightness scores. Measured bend-based stem descriptions were about twice as precise as subjective scores. — [Benyon thesis, ANU](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstream/1885/143322/2/b1748067x_Benyon_R_G.pdf)
- Queensland slash pine: tree size (height) explained only about 25% of the variation in in-grade structural recovery. Sweep and taper are cited as affecting where cuts fall, but sweep was not quantified as an outturn predictor. — [FWPA PN06.3016](https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/3144/2/PN06.3016_Slash_pine_Resource_Evaluation_2009%5B1%5D-sec.pdf)

### Inferences
- In practice the code-to-outturn conversion is done by the bucking optimiser (YTGEN or equivalent), using the buyer's log specs: SED-fraction sweep per product and length. Anchoring PS_LiDAR to Australia means plugging HQP's current product sweep limits into that step. For Queensland the realistic public proxies are the ForestrySA ladder (sawlog SED/4, industrial SED/3, preservation SED/5, pulp SED/1–1.5), but they are radiata / SA-specific.
- A sensible, citable fallback hierarchy for thresholds, until HQP specs are obtained:
  1. HQP log spec (to obtain).
  2. ForestrySA SLSD002 and export chart (Australian softwood precedent).
  3. NZ MPI / Canopy sweep classes (length-banded SED fractions).
  4. The 1 cm/m family (UK FC, RVR, Finland) as an independent check.

### Gaps
- No Australian (Queensland PHYB, slash or Caribbean) study linking cruise sweep codes or LiDAR-measured sweep to sawn or peeled recovery was found. FWPA project reports on southern pine resource characterisation (e.g. PNC361-1416) were seen in search results but not examined for sweep–outturn relationships.
- HQP's product mix and its customers' sweep tolerances are not public. PN06.3016 names Hyne and Weyerhaeuser as sawmill processors of the slash pine study logs in 2008, but current customer specs (sawmill, plywood or LVL peeler, preservation, pulp) were not found.
